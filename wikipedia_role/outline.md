# Essay outline: what is owed to the community behind Wikipedia

Working draft, 3 October 2026. Status: proposed, not yet confirmed by the author. Sections marked **[decision]** need the author's choice.

## Working title

*The last bastion is a community: Wikipedia, language models, and what licence-compliant reuse leaves out*

## Research question

**Main question.** If AI developers' reuse of Wikipedia complies with its licence, what, if anything, do they owe to the community that produces it, and what does the answer show about what makes Wikipedia worth defending in the era of large language models?

**Sub-questions**

1. *What is the bastion?* In what sense is Wikipedia a "source of truth" in the LLM era: its content, its verification process, or its community? Why did its standing change from disparaged to relied upon?
2. *What is owed?* Can reuse that breaches no licence still wrong the community, and in which sense: betrayal, exploitation, free-riding, or neglect of a shared dependency?
3. *What should change?* What follows for Wikipedia's own practices and for the terms on which it is reused?

## Thesis (proposed)

1. **Relational legitimacy (T3).** Wikipedia's standing as a source of truth is relational. It rose when the alternatives were distrusted or collapsed, and it never claimed truth, only verifiability. What is worth defending is not a body of content but a community-held practice of verification: knowledge that, in Hardwig's terms, is known by the community and not by any individual.
2. **Something is owed that the licence does not say (T2).** The common reaction that reuse by AI developers is a betrayal of the community is a loose way of naming a real unease. Taken as a technical charge it would need a commitment held by the party accused (Hawley, 2014), and for general reuse under an open licence there is mostly none. The essay uses the commitment account briefly, to show why that word fits few cases, and then asks what the unease tracks.
3. **What it tracks is a duty of stewardship.** Those who depend on a shared epistemic infrastructure, and whose reuse strains it, owe something toward keeping it going. This is better located in dependence, fair play and the avoidance of exploitation than in a broken promise.
4. **The most urgent change is internal.** If the bastion is the community, the main threat to it is the one the literature documents from inside, the loss of newcomers and of inclusion, not only extraction from outside.

**Settled (author, 3 October 2026): "betrayal" is colloquial, not a technical claim.** The essay therefore does not defend betrayal. It treats the word as an everyday name for the unease, tests it briefly in section 5, and builds the argument on a stewardship duty (option B in the earlier draft).

## Structure and word budget (target about 9,000 words; check the target journal's limit)

### 1. Introduction (about 900)

- The reversal: scorned in classrooms, then invoked as the antidote to hallucination, then drained of readers by the systems that rely on it.
- Puzzle and thesis. What the essay does not claim: that Wikipedia is failing, or that AI use of it is illegal.
- *Sources:* Jaschik (2007); Waters (2007); Wikimedia Foundation (2025d); Miller (2025).

### 2. Three platforms, three fates (about 900)

- What "dead" can mean: contributions, attention, business model.
- Stack Overflow's questions collapse; Reddit grows and explores licensing its archive; Wikipedia loses attention yet stays large. The comparison sets the empirical premises and corrects the common claim that Reddit is dead.
- *Evidence:* Stack Exchange API pull; Reddit 10-K; del Rio-Chanona et al. (2024); Burtch et al. (2024); Reeves et al. (2025) versus Lyu et al. (2025); Wikimedia pageviews and Foundation reports.
- *Caveat to state:* aggregate readership decline is about 7-8% a year, and the academic evidence on contributions is mixed.

### 3. What the bastion is (T3) (about 2,000)

- 3.1 *Verifiability, not truth.* What Wikipedia claims to be; why "source of truth" is an external label (Wikipedia contributors, n.d.; Garfinkel, 2008).
- 3.2 *Legitimacy by comparison.* Fallis's comparative defence; Giles's parity result; the succession pattern (Phaedrus, retrieval, panic cycles) and its weak empirical base (Sparrow et al., 2011 versus Camerer et al., 2018). The argument is conceptual: no study tests relative legitimacy.
- 3.3 *The community as knower.* Hardwig (community knowledge, chains of authority); Menking and Rosenberg (process over product); Halfaker et al. (verification machinery thins the community).
- 3.4 *Whose bastion.* Geography, gender, notability, oral knowledge, Vietnamese edition (Graham et al., 2014; Tripodi, 2023; Gallert & van der Velden, 2014; Miquel-Ribé & Laniado, 2018). Defences: Hill and Shaw (2013); Shi et al. (2019).
- *Claim of the section:* the bastion is a verification practice carried by people, and its walls have known gaps.

### 4. What licence-compliant reuse leaves out (about 1,200)

- 4.1 What the licence promises: CC BY-SA text; Wikimedia Terms of Use; Creative Commons on machine reuse; fair use scholarship; *Doe v. GitHub* (as reported); the Open Source Definition.
- 4.2 Harms outside the licence: attention and referral traffic; bot load on infrastructure (Mueller et al., 2025); contributor effects (mixed evidence); the Foundation's own requests for credit and payment.
- *Claim:* the licence is the wrong instrument for the worry, as its own steward concedes. The harm is real but modest and unevenly evidenced.

### 5. What the unease tracks (about 1,800)

- 5.1 *Why "betrayal" is the wrong word for most cases.* Hawley's commitment account in brief: commitment may be implicit; expectations without commitment give a feeling of betrayal, not a betrayal; commitments to third parties. Candidate commitments (licence attribution duty, the Foundation's requests, public assurances such as Stack Overflow, 2024) run to different parties, and where one was made and not kept the word can apply.
- 5.2 *The hard case: a gift to anyone* (Mauss; Open Source Definition), and whether convention was disowned.
- 5.3 *Better descriptions:* free-riding on a cooperative scheme (Hart; Rawls, with the participation condition); exploitation that is consensual yet unfair (Zwolinski et al.); neglect of a dependency; data labour and leverage.
- *Claim:* the unease is well founded but is not about a broken promise; it is about reliance without upkeep.

### 6. What is owed instead: stewardship of an epistemic commons (about 1,400)

- Dependence: AI relies on human-held, verified knowledge (Hardwig; Shumailov et al., 2024) with the contested nature of that dependence stated (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024; Villalobos et al., 2024).
- Commons governance: Ostrom's rules, Huang and Siddarth's "contribute back", Longpre et al. on consent.
- Forms of reciprocity assessed against whom each commitment runs to: attribution, referral traffic, paid access (Enterprise: $8.3 million in fiscal 2024-25, terms undisclosed), preference signals, funding contributions.
- *Claim:* a duty of stewardship is defensible without a broken promise, and it names what to ask of developers.

### 7. What Wikipedia should change (about 900)

- Say plainly what it offers: auditability (provenance, contestability, accountability), not truth.
- Treat newcomer retention and inclusion as the central defence of the bastion (Halfaker et al., 2013; Menking & Rosenberg, 2021).
- Transparency of reuse agreements; the risk of a commercial model (Becker, 2026).

### 8. Objections and replies (about 700)

- A gift to anyone cannot be betrayed.
- AI answers may serve readers better, so who is wronged?
- Wikipedia is financially healthy.
- "Last bastion" is romantic: of whose knowledge?
- Contamination: current evidence of LLM text inside Wikipedia is modest (Huang et al., 2025; Brooks et al., 2024; working data in `ai_text/`).

## What would change the argument

- Evidence that editors do hold, and AI developers knowingly encouraged, a reciprocity expectation would give the commitment account more work to do and would strengthen the case for an owed duty.
- Evidence that Wikimedia's reuse agreements deliver credit, traffic and funding in practice would weaken the case for an outstanding duty.
- A study showing a general collapse in contributions caused by AI would strengthen the harm premise in section 4.

## FINER check of the main question

- *Feasible:* yes, as a conceptual essay on public documents and the reviewed literature; an empirical survey of editors would be a separate project.
- *Interesting:* the reversal in Wikipedia's standing and the licence-versus-obligation gap are both live.
- *Novel:* the review found no work linking social epistemology to LLM reuse of Wikipedia, none testing relative legitimacy, none applying the Fricker framework to Wikipedia, and none applying a commitment account of betrayal to open licences.
- *Ethical:* no human subjects.
- *Relevant:* bears on the policy debate over AI reuse of open knowledge.
