## Abstract

Wikipedia, once barred from classroom citation, is now invoked as a safeguard against machine-generated falsehood, even as its human readership falls and the firms that train language models on its text owe nothing under its licence by way of return. This essay asks what Wikipedia is for in the era of language models. It argues that Wikipedia's standing as a source of truth is relational, and that what deserves defence is a community-held practice of verification whose distinctive property is auditability: provenance, contestability and answerability, valued because they serve error-correction and calibrated trust. Language models rely on the practice, the licence is silent about that reliance, and the strain on the practice is modest and contested. Fair play, natural duty and beneficence each point toward, but none establishes, a duty on reusers; at most they give a light, defeasible reason to contribute to upkeep, which can take several forms. The practice's role depends more on what Wikipedia does itself, and the essay sets out changes, on provenance that travels with reused content, on newcomers, on what counts as knowledge, and on openness about reuse agreements, whose force does not depend on whether reusers contribute. The argument is conceptual, draws on public documents and the scholarly literature, and marks where the evidence is thin or comes from an interested party.

**Keywords:** Wikipedia; large language models; knowledge commons; social epistemology; auditability; stewardship
