# Lit 6: Critiques and limits of Wikipedia as an epistemic institution

Verification notes: publisher pages (Sage, Nature, Springer, Taylor & Francis, MIT Press) mostly returned 403 or login redirects. Bibliographic details were therefore confirmed through Crossref API records, Semantic Scholar, ORA, arXiv, PLOS, Cambridge Core, Frontiers and the Wikimedia Commons PDF. Annotations rest on abstracts or summaries only unless stated. None of the 14 sources was read in full except where marked.

## A. Critiques

### 1. Halfaker, Geiger, Morgan and Riedl (2013)
Halfaker, A., Geiger, R. S., Morgan, J. T., & Riedl, J. (2013). The rise and decline of an open collaboration system: How Wikipedia's reaction to popularity is causing its decline. *American Behavioral Scientist, 57*(5), 664-688. https://doi.org/10.1177/0002764212469365
- Type: peer-reviewed article (online 2012, print issue 2013)
- Verification: Crossref record for the DOI (authors, title, volume, issue, pages match).
- Annotation (abstract only): Argues that Wikipedia's contributor base shrank after 2007 even as content grew. The authors present evidence that quality-control mechanisms and algorithmic tools used to reject contributions are key causes of lower newcomer retention. Governance structures built to manage growth became resistant to newer editors' input.
- Bearing: The bastion's verification machinery is itself a hole. The defences that protect quality also thin the community that is said to be the source of its authority.
- Keywords: editor retention, newcomers, bots, quality control, governance
- **Full-text update (3 Oct 2026, copy supplied by the author):** read in full (25 pages; ABS 57(5), 664-688; online 2012). Active English editors peaked in March 2007 at 56,400; the authors reject the "right-sizing" explanation because most articles remain below community standards for good articles and underrepresented groups still find joining hard. They show a steady inflow of "desirable newcomers" with falling survival, tie it to restrictive quality control and algorithmic rejection tools, and show formal norm-articulation mechanisms calcifying against changes proposed by newer editors.

### 2. Graham, Hogan, Straumann and Medhat (2014)
Graham, M., Hogan, B., Straumann, R. K., & Medhat, A. (2014). Uneven geographies of user-generated information: Patterns of increasing informational poverty. *Annals of the Association of American Geographers, 104*(4), 746-764. https://doi.org/10.1080/00045608.2014.910087
- Type: peer-reviewed article
- Verification: Crossref search record (DOI, volume, pages) plus abstract on Oxford University Research Archive (ORA). Note that the DOI ending 892944 is wrong and returned 404.
- Annotation (abstract only): Uses geotagged Wikipedia content to ask whether wider internet connectivity has democratised knowledge production. The encyclopedia shows uneven, clustered geographies, with little content about much of the world. Connectivity alone does not fix this, because social, economic, political, regulatory and infrastructural barriers persist, and some regions fall below levels predicted by explanatory factors.
- Bearing: Shows whose places and knowledge are well covered. The bastion's walls enclose a map with large blanks.
- Keywords: geography, informational periphery, digital divide, geotagged articles, Oxford Internet Institute
- **Full-text update (3 Oct 2026, copy supplied by the author):** read in full (20 pages; Annals of the AAG 104(4), 746-764). Confirms the abstract. Adds that a model with a few conditions explained 71% of the variance in geographies of information while some regions stayed well below expected values; it places special focus on the Middle East and North Africa and sub-Saharan Africa; it reports, citing Silverwood-Cope (2012), that Wikipedia appeared on the first page of 99% of 1,000 Google searches for nouns, and that 15% of Internet users accessed it on a given day (figures as of the paper's date). The correct DOI is 10.1080/00045608.2014.910087.

### 3. Wagner, Graells-Garrido, Garcia and Menczer (2016)
Wagner, C., Graells-Garrido, E., Garcia, D., & Menczer, F. (2016). Women through the glass ceiling: Gender asymmetries in Wikipedia. *EPJ Data Science, 5*, Article 5. https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-016-0066-4
- Type: peer-reviewed article (open access)
- Verification: Crossref search record for DOI, title, authors, volume; Semantic Scholar record with summary. Article number "5" is from memory of the journal's numbering and was not seen. Check before citing.
- Annotation (Semantic Scholar summary only): Examines gender asymmetries in structure, content, lexical choice and notability. Women in Wikipedia are more notable on average than men, read as a subtle glass-ceiling effect. Women's biographies skew toward family and relationship themes, and linguistic bias appears in descriptors.
- Bearing: The subject-side counterpart to the editor gap. It shows that inclusion thresholds are not neutral.
- Keywords: gender gap, biographies, notability, language bias

### 4. Ford and Wajcman (2017)
Ford, H., & Wajcman, J. (2017). 'Anyone can edit', not everyone does: Wikipedia's infrastructure and the gender gap. *Social Studies of Science, 47*(4), 511-527. https://doi.org/10.1177/0306312717692172
- Type: peer-reviewed article
- Verification: Crossref API record (authors, title, volume, issue, pages, abstract). Sage page returned 403.
- Annotation (abstract only): Takes up the gender disparity in editing, with fewer than ten percent of editors women. Beyond masculine technoscience culture, the authors argue that Wikipedia's infrastructure introduces new, less visible sources of gender disparity.
- Bearing: The "anyone can edit" premise is formally true and practically false. The openness that grounds the bastion's legitimacy is uneven by design.
- Keywords: gender gap, infrastructure, STS, openness
- **Full-text update (3 Oct 2026, copy supplied by the author):** read in full (17 pages; pp. 511-527). Argues that Wikipedia's origins and infrastructure rest on epistemologies that exclude women; that being a Wikipedian means acquiring sociotechnical expertise that is gender-coded male; and that those who master its technocratic system of representation, with its emphasis on facts and modular verifiable information, become power brokers. Notes that search-engine fact boxes black-box Wikipedia's facts. Concludes that unless Wikipedia changes its culture of knowledge production, women's knowledge will again be marginalised. The framing is feminist STS.

### 5. Menking and Rosenberg (2021)
Menking, A., & Rosenberg, J. (2021). WP:NOT, WP:NPOV, and other stories Wikipedia tells us: A feminist critique of Wikipedia's epistemology. *Science, Technology, & Human Values, 46*(3), 455-479. https://doi.org/10.1177/0162243920924783
- Type: peer-reviewed article (online 2020, issue 2021)
- Verification: Crossref API record (matching authors, title, volume, issue, pages, abstract). PhilPapers page returned 403.
- Annotation (abstract only): Argues that Wikipedia's five pillars, including neutral point of view, support the gender gap and lack of diversity in participation and content. Draws on Lorraine Code's epistemic responsibility and Helen Longino's procedural objectivity. Proposes alternative pillars aimed at a more robust, reliable and transparent knowledge-production site.
- Bearing: The closest I found to an epistemology-level critique of NPOV. It treats the policies as gatekeepers of what counts as knowledge, which is the essay's question.
- Keywords: NPOV, five pillars, feminist epistemology, procedural objectivity
- **Full-text update (3 Oct 2026, copy supplied by the author):** read in full. Abstract confirmed. The authors argue that the first pillar prioritises the product over the process of knowledge production and propose reframing the pillars around the process of inquiry (the "back end" of editor disputes about sources and reliability). They write that Wikipedia is "more than just an encyclopedia". They report that editors who challenged the pillars have been punished (topic bans in December 2014). On p. 464 they say they considered Fricker's epistemic injustice and Harding's standpoint epistemology and decided against them because those approaches may be less compatible with, or less sympathetic to, "the inherently conservative encyclopedic project". So Fricker is mentioned and deliberately set aside.

### 6. Tripodi (2023)
Tripodi, F. (2023). Ms. Categorized: Gender, notability, and inequality on Wikipedia. *New Media & Society, 25*(7), 1687-1707. https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448211023772
- Type: peer-reviewed article (online 2021)
- Verification: CITAP (UNC) publication page gives DOI, title and findings. Volume and pages come from a search result, not the page. Confirm in the journal.
- Annotation (from the CITAP summary): Observed edit-a-thons, interviewed editors and analysed over 20,000 "articles for deletion" biographies. Women's biographies are nominated for deletion more often than their share of biographies, and women editors bear extra emotional labour defending them. The apparently neutral application of notability roles embeds subjectivity and bias.
- Bearing: A direct case of a verification process (notability, deletion debate) producing exclusion. It also supports a Fricker-style reading of credibility deficit, though the article does not use that framework as far as I saw.
- Keywords: notability, deletion, gender, emotional labour
- **Full-text update (3 Oct 2026, copy supplied by the author):** read in full. Volume, issue and pages confirmed (New Media & Society 25(7), 1687-1707). Ethnographic observation of edit-a-thons, interviews (about 60% women, 40% men; 36 originally, three withdrew), and 38 months (Jan 2017 to Feb 2020) of deletion-nomination metadata on 22,174 biographies. Women's share of biographies rose from 16.83% to 18.25% while women were consistently over 25% of nominated biographies. Of nominated biographies that were kept, about 25% of women's and 17% of men's (19% overall); "keep" is read as a marker of miscategorisation, which is an inference. She cites Adams et al. (2019), who found women academics were not more likely to be deleted. Fricker and "epistemic" do not appear in the text.

### 7. Miquel-Ribé and Laniado (2018)
Miquel-Ribé, M., & Laniado, D. (2018). Wikipedia culture gap: Quantifying content imbalances across 40 language editions. *Frontiers in Physics, 6*, Article 54. https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2018.00054
- Type: peer-reviewed article (open access)
- Verification: Opened the Frontiers full-text page.
- Annotation (page summary): Computational method identifies culturally contextual content across 40 language editions. About a quarter of each edition covers its own cultural context, and that content is about 4.15 times less shared across languages than average content. Vietnamese Wikipedia (about 1.14 million articles at the time) had 2.5 percent; correction of 5 October 2026 after reading the paper's Table 2 and text: Cebuano and Waray were lower, at 0.1 percent each, so Vietnamese was third lowest of the 40 (mean 23.2%, median 24.2%), and the authors name all three as editions where bots had a major role in creation and translation.
- Bearing: Small and mid-sized editions are not independent bastions. The Vietnamese case suggests large article counts can hide thin local knowledge. This is the only Vietnamese-specific quantitative evidence found.
- Keywords: language editions, cultural context, Vietnamese Wikipedia, bots, coverage gap

### 8. Gallert and van der Velden (2014 chapter, read as preprint)
Gallert, P., & van der Velden, M. (2014). Reliable sources for indigenous knowledge: Dissecting Wikipedia's catch-22. In N. J. Bidwell & H. Winschiers-Theophilus (Eds.), *Indigenous Knowledge Technology Conference (IKTC) 2011 post-conference book*. Unedited preliminary version: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/51/Indigenous_Knowledge_for_Wikipedia.pdf
- Type: chapter (the version I read is a preprint, marked as unedited)
- Verification: Downloaded the Commons PDF and extracted its text. Title, both authors and the book framing match. I could not confirm final publisher, pages or DOI, so the citation is incomplete.
- Annotation (read the opening section): Wikipedia is rooted in a culture of writing and restricts itself to facts from reliable, published, third-party sources defined by its editors. Oral transmission is not counted as publishing, knowledge keepers are judged too close to their subjects for neutrality, and songs and stories are not seen as reliable preservation. The authors say editors are mostly educated men from the northern hemisphere, and what is reliable or neutral is set by their consensus.
- Bearing: The sharpest statement of the policy catch-22. Following the rules faithfully excludes the knowledge the project says it wants.
- Keywords: indigenous knowledge, oral sources, reliable sources, NPOV, Namibia

## B. Defences and qualifications

### 11. Hill and Shaw (2013)
Hill, B. M., & Shaw, A. (2013). The Wikipedia gender gap revisited: Characterizing survey response bias with propensity score estimation. *PLOS ONE, 8*(6), e65782. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0065782
- Type: peer-reviewed article
- Verification: Opened the PLOS ONE page (authors, title, DOI match). Issue and article number come from the DOI, which I recognise but did not see on the page.
- Annotation (abstract): Re-analyses the 2008 Wikimedia survey that reported under 13 percent of contributors were female. Combining it with a representative Pew survey, they estimate the share of women at 16.1 percent globally and 22.7 percent for US adults, about 27 percent higher than first reported.
- Bearing: A qualification rather than a refutation. The gap is real but smaller than the headline figure, so the holes should be measured carefully. The gap persists even after correction.
- Keywords: gender gap, survey bias, propensity score

### 12. Shi, Teplitskiy, Duede and Evans (2019)
Shi, F., Teplitskiy, M., Duede, E., & Evans, J. A. (2019). The wisdom of polarized crowds. *Nature Human Behaviour, 3*(4), 329-336. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-019-0541-6 (arXiv preprint: arXiv:1712.06414)
- Type: peer-reviewed article. The arXiv version is a preprint.
- Verification: Opened the arXiv abstract page, which names the Nature Human Behaviour version and DOI. Volume and pages come from a search result, not the Nature page (redirected to login).
- Annotation (abstract only): Studies hundreds of thousands of Wikipedia editorial teams. Politically balanced, polarised teams produce higher-quality articles than homogeneous ones. Diverse teams hold longer, more substantive talk-page debates and rely more on institutional policies.
- Bearing: A defence of the process. Diversity of editors plus policy discipline is what makes the bastion work. It also implies that narrower demographics (sources 3, 4, 6) weaken it.
- Keywords: ideological diversity, article quality, talk pages, collaboration

### 13. Tollefsen (2009)
Tollefsen, D. P. (2009). Wikipedia and the epistemology of testimony. *Episteme, 6*(1), 8-24. https://doi.org/10.3366/E1742360008000518
- Type: peer-reviewed article
- Verification: Opened the Cambridge Core abstract page (author, title, volume, pages, DOI match).
- Annotation (abstract only): Asks whether Wikipedia entries are individual testimony or collective testimony from the community. Extends reductionist accounts of testimonial justification to group testimony and discusses how to assess the trustworthiness of a collective epistemic source.
- Bearing: Gives the philosophical frame for treating Wikipedia as a collective witness whose trust depends on its processes. It is the nearest analytic-philosophy anchor found for the testimony question, though it predates the bias literature and does not apply Fricker.
- Keywords: testimony, collective epistemic agency, trust

### 14. Jemielniak (2014)
Jemielniak, D. (2014). *Common knowledge? An ethnography of Wikipedia*. Stanford University Press.
- Type: book
- Verification: Google Books record (author, title, publisher, year, description). The De Gruyter page returned 405.
- Annotation (publisher description only): An ethnography by an active Wikipedian. It argues that Wikipedia is not anarchic. Open access and power coexist in a distinctive bureaucracy with administrators, procedures and hierarchy. Topics include conflict resolution, consensus and governance.
- Bearing: Describes the governance and power among editors that a "community" defence glosses over. It is sympathetic but not naive, so it works as both critic and defender.
- Keywords: governance, bureaucracy, administrators, ethnography

## Gaps and disputes

I found no verified paper that applies Fricker's framework to Wikipedia directly. Sources 5, 6, 8 and 13 supply the materials for that argument, but the essay would be making the application itself. Vietnamese-language evidence is thin: only source 7 covers it, and its claims about bot translation need checking against Wikimedia data. Not covered, because I could not verify content: paid editing and manipulation (Beutler's MIT Press chapter exists but I could not read it), vandalism studies, and Giles's 2005 Nature comparison with Britannica, which I confirmed only by metadata. Disputed: the size of the gender gap (source 11 versus 4) and whether deletion bias or editor bias drives it.
