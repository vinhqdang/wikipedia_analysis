# Thread 5: Empirical and policy facts on Wikipedia and LLMs, 2023-2026

All sources accessed 3 October 2026. "Verified" means the page itself (or the API/registry record named) was opened and author/issuer, date and title were read. Page contents were read through a fetch tool that summarises pages; quoted wording is as returned by that tool, so exact quotes should be rechecked on the page before publication. Numbers I could not trace to a primary or peer-reviewed source are flagged "AGGREGATOR ONLY".

## A. Wikimedia Foundation primary sources

**1. Miller, M. (2025). "New User Trends on Wikipedia." Wikimedia Diff, 17 Oct 2025. https://diff.wikimedia.org/2025/10/17/new-user-trends-on-wikipedia/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary report (WMF blog). Verification: opened page; author Marshall Miller, dated 17 Oct 2025.
Annotation: Reports "a decrease of roughly 8% as compared to the same months in 2024" in human pageviews after bot-detection was revised. States that much of the unusually high May-June 2025 traffic "was coming from bots that were built to evade detection". Attributes the fall to generative-AI answers in search ("Search engines are increasingly using generative AI to provide answers directly to searchers rather than linking to sites like ours"). Also: "Almost all large language models (LLMs) train on Wikipedia datasets."
Bears on: core T3 evidence (role as destination shrinking) and T2 (content used, readers not returned). Complication: 8% is a year-on-year dip, and it rests on a reclassification of earlier bot traffic, so earlier "growth" was partly artefact.

**2. Mueller, B., Danis, C., Lavagetto, G. (2025). "How crawlers impact the operations of the Wikimedia projects." Wikimedia Diff, 1 Apr 2025. https://diff.wikimedia.org/2025/04/01/how-crawlers-impact-the-operations-of-the-wikimedia-projects/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary report (WMF engineering blog). Verification: opened; authors and date as shown.
Annotation: Bots generate 65% of the most resource-intensive traffic to core datacentres while accounting for only 35% of pageviews. Multimedia bandwidth consumption rose 50% since January 2024, attributed mainly to scrapers collecting AI training data. Commons hosts 144 million files. WMF frames a "Responsible Use of Infrastructure" response.
Bears on: T2 directly (costs borne by WMF for AI firms' scraping).

**3. Wikimedia Enterprise (2026). "Announcing New Wikimedia Enterprise Partners for Wikipedia's 25th Birthday." 15 Jan 2026. https://enterprise.wikimedia.com/blog/wikipedia-25-enterprise-partners/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary announcement. Verification: opened; dated 15 Jan 2026, no named author.
Annotation: Names Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral AI and Perplexity as partners, alongside Google, Ecosia, Nomic, Pleias, ProRata and Reef Media. States that audiences view "more than 65 million articles in over 300 languages nearly 15 billion times every month." No contract values are given.
Bears on: T2. Complicates a "no reciprocity" claim: large firms now pay for access, but terms are undisclosed and the announcement says the partnerships were formalised earlier (via trade press, not verified from a primary page).

**4. Wikimedia Foundation (2026). "Wikipedia celebrates 25 years of knowledge at its best." News release, 15 Jan 2026. https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/01/15/wikipedia-celebrates-25years/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary press release. Verification: opened; dated 15 Jan 2026.
Annotation: "Nearly 15 billion" monthly views, over 65 million articles, nearly 250,000 monthly contributors, 1.5 billion unique devices a month. Lists Ecosia, Microsoft, Mistral AI, Perplexity, Pleias and ProRata as new Enterprise partners and Amazon, Google and Meta as existing. CEO line: "knowledge is human, and knowledge needs humans".
Bears on: T3. Complicates decline: scale remains very large (about 15 billion views a month). Note the editor count here (~250,000) differs from the 273,000 in source 7.

**5. Wikimedia Foundation (2025). "Wikimedia Enterprise Financial Report: Fiscal Year 2024-2025." Wikimedia Diff, 24 Nov 2025. https://diff.wikimedia.org/2025/11/24/wikimedia-enterprise-financial-report-fiscal-year-2024-2025/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary financial report. Verification: opened; dated 24 Nov 2025.
Annotation: Enterprise revenue $8.3M, up 148% from $3.4M; expenses $4.4M; net profit $3.854M (against a $432K loss the year before); 4.0% of WMF revenue; "13 commercial customers utilising the paid tier"; cumulative net profit $646K since January 2022 launch.
Bears on: T2. Quantifies reciprocity: about $8.3M a year in a context of $208.6M total revenue is small relative to the AI sector's use of the content.

**6. Wikimedia Foundation (2025). "Highlights from the Wikimedia Foundation's fiscal year 2024-2025 audit report." Wikimedia Diff, 24 Nov 2025. https://diff.wikimedia.org/2025/11/24/highlights-from-the-wikimedia-foundations-fiscal-year-2024-2025-audit-report/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary financial report. Verification: opened; dated 24 Nov 2025, FY 1 Jul 2024-30 Jun 2025.
Annotation: Total revenue $208.6M, of which $189.5M donations; revenue 11% above the $188.7M target; expenses up 7% ($12.4M) to $190.9M; clean KPMG audit.
Bears on: complicates a "Wikipedia is declining" story. The institution's finances grew in the same year traffic fell. (Donation breakdown figures of $62.4M banner / 30% came only from a search summary of the annual report, not read on the page.)

**7. Wikimedia Foundation (2026). "Introducing the Wikimedia Foundation's draft FY 2026-2027 Annual Plan." Wikimedia Diff, 27 Apr 2026. https://diff.wikimedia.org/2026/04/27/introducing-the-wikimedia-foundations-draft-fy-2026-2027-annual-plan/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary planning document. Verification: opened; dated 27 Apr 2026.
Annotation: "Wikipedia is seeing declines in pageviews and referral traffic from Google, and unprecedented increases in bot traffic." Nearly 90% of visitors historically arrived via Google. About 273,000 editors, "0.02% of the 1.5 billion monthly Wikipedia readers." Proposes "a sustainable reuse model that ... sends value and future contributors back to Wikipedia—not just traffic away."
Bears on: T2 and T3, in WMF's own words. The reciprocity ask is stated as an aim, not a delivered fact.

**8. Wikimedia Foundation (2026). "Wikimedia Foundation Annual Plan/2026-2027/Global Trends." Meta-Wiki, last edited 9 May 2026. https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation_Annual_Plan/2026-2027/Global_Trends (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary planning page (wiki, editable). Verification: opened; last-edit date 9 May 2026.
Annotation: Qualitative only (no pageview or editor figures). Notes declining traffic from AI summaries, "AI-generated forks of human-created content" including commercial alternatives to Wikipedia, and a risk that paid opportunities for training commercial AI diverts contributors from open knowledge projects.
Bears on: T3 (displacement by derivative AI encyclopaedias) and T2.

**9. Wikimedia Foundation (2025/26). "Wikipedia year in review 2025." https://wikimediafoundation.org/wikipedia-year-in-review-2025/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary report. Verification: opened; page shows December 2025, updated January 2026.
Annotation: 94,135,666 edits across all languages in 2025; about 324 edits a minute; nearly 250,000 editors (data to 30 June 2025); English unique devices 965 million a month; 4.15 billion bytes added to English Wikipedia; 2.76 billion reading hours in English.
Bears on: complicates decline. Edit and readership volumes remain very high.

## B. Community policy

**10. English Wikipedia (2026). "Wikipedia:Writing articles with large language models" (WP:LLM). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Writing_articles_with_large_language_models (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: policy page (content guideline). Verification: opened; edit history shows live edits 20-26 Sep 2026.
Annotation: States that "The use of large language models (LLMs) to generate or rewrite article content is prohibited", with exceptions for copyediting one's own writing and for translation, with human review. Persistent violation counts as disruptive editing. The RfC behind it passed 44 to 2 and closed 20 March 2026 (source 11).
Bears on: T3. The community is defending human authorship. Note it is a guideline whose wording is still being edited.

**11. Bansal, A. (2026). "Wikipedia bans AI-generated article content." MediaNama, 26 Mar 2026. https://www.medianama.com/2026/03/223-english-wikipedia-bans-ai-generated-text-allows-limited-use-copyediting-translation/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: news-commentary. Verification: opened; author and date as shown.
Annotation: RfC passed "with 44 votes in favor and 2 opposed", closing 20 March 2026. Reports that Wikipedia acknowledges AI detection tools "are currently unreliable" and describes an autonomous agent (TomWikiAssist) that authored several articles in early March 2026.
Bears on: dates the policy; the 44-2 vote is a small-sample community measure, not a population estimate.

**12. English Wikipedia. "Wikipedia:Criteria for speedy deletion", criterion G15. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Criteria_for_speedy_deletion (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: policy page. Verification: opened; G15 text read.
Annotation: G15 covers pages with signs of un-reviewed LLM generation in three categories: communication aimed at the user (e.g. "Here is your Wikipedia article on..."), non-existent or nonsensical references, and technical artefacts such as `oaicite` or `attributionIndex` strings. Other signs, including admitted LLM use, do not qualify and go through other processes.
Bears on: shows deliberately narrow, evidence-based enforcement. (Adoption date not retrieved from the page; see source 16 for the governance history.)

**13. WikiProject AI Cleanup. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:WikiProject_AI_Cleanup and "Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing (both accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: policy/project pages (the second is an essay-style advice page). Verification: both opened.
Annotation: The project was founded 4 Dec 2023 by three editors and had over 300 registered participants on the page I read. "Signs of AI writing" calls itself "a field guide to help detect undisclosed AI-generated content" and says it "is not an encyclopedia article or a Wikipedia policy, as it has not been reviewed by the community." It warns that indicators are not proof because LLMs "are trained on human writing, including Wikipedia".
Bears on: T3. Shows volunteers acting as a filter on AI text. The project's own page also notes it covers suspected AI images and agents.

## C. Academic studies

**14. Brooks, C., Eggert, S., Peskoff, D. (2024). "The Rise of AI-Generated Content in Wikipedia." Proceedings of the First Workshop on Advancing NLP for Wikipedia (WikiNLP), Miami, Nov 2024, pp. 67-79. https://aclanthology.org/2024.wikinlp-1.12/ (accessed 3 Oct 2026); preprint arXiv:2410.08044**
Type: peer-reviewed workshop paper. Verification: opened ACL Anthology page; authors, venue, date.
Annotation: Using GPTZero and Binoculars, detectors "flag over 5% of newly created English Wikipedia articles" as AI-generated, with lower rates in German, French and Italian. Flagged articles were typically lower quality and more promotional or biased on divisive topics. The authors present these as lower bounds, with detector error acknowledged.
Bears on: T3 (contamination of the knowledge base) and, indirectly, on the "last bastion" premise. Complication: detector-based estimates of recent articles only, with a short window.

**15. Lyu, L., Siderius, J., Li, H., Acemoglu, D., Huttenlocher, D., Ozdaglar, A. (2025). "Wikipedia Contributions in the Wake of ChatGPT." The Web Conference (WWW) 2025; arXiv:2503.00757, 2 Mar 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00757 (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: peer-reviewed conference paper (WWW 2025; venue as stated on arXiv page). Verification: opened arXiv page.
Annotation: Difference-in-differences: "newly created, popular articles whose content overlaps with ChatGPT 3.5 saw a greater decline in editing and viewership" than dissimilar ones. A search summary of the paper's text (not read on the page) put the view effect at about 29,013 fewer views a month and the edit effect at about 6.8 fewer edits, with the effect concentrated in articles under 10 months old; treat those two figures as unverified until the PDF is checked.
Bears on: T3, strongest causal-style evidence of substitution. Complicates: effect is on new articles, with negligible edit effect on older ones.

**16. Reeves, N., Yin, W., Simperl, E. (2024). "Exploring the Impact of ChatGPT on Wikipedia Engagement." arXiv:2405.10205, submitted 16 May 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10205 (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: preprint. Verification: opened arXiv page.
Annotation: Across twelve language versions after ChatGPT's November 2022 release, "page views and visitor numbers increased"; no evidence of reduced page views, unique visitors, edit counts or editor numbers. Growth was lower in languages where ChatGPT was available than in those without it.
Bears on: COMPLICATES T3. Through early 2024 there was no measurable decline; the later fall (source 1) came after this study window.

**17. Huang, S., Xu, Y., Geng, M., Wan, Y., Chen, D. (2025; rev. 2 Mar 2026). "Wikipedia in the Era of LLMs: Evolution and Risks." arXiv:2503.02879; accepted by TMLR. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02879 (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: preprint, accepted at TMLR per the arXiv page. Verification: opened.
Annotation: Estimates LLM-related change in Wikipedia content at "approximately 1% in certain categories" and flags three risks: benchmark contamination, degraded retrieval-augmented generation, and knowledge contamination.
Bears on: COMPLICATES T3. Measured LLM influence on content is small (about 1% in some categories), set against Brooks et al.'s over 5% of new articles.

**18. Froneman, W. (2026). "Failed comprehensiveness, successful minimalism: Wikipedia's 3-year struggle to govern AI-generated content (2022-2025)." AI & Society, published 25 Apr 2026. DOI 10.1007/s00146-026-03046-1, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-026-03046-1 (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: peer-reviewed article. Verification: Springer page blocked by redirect; record and abstract confirmed through the Crossref API (title, sole author, journal, date).
Annotation: Process-tracing of revision histories and RfCs. Two comprehensive policy proposals failed; the community adopted a speedy-deletion criterion for un-reviewed AI-generated pages and a one-sentence guideline against generating new articles from scratch. Argues for a "comprehensiveness trap" in consensus governance. (A search summary also mentioned over 4,800 articles flagged by late 2025; I did not read this in the paper, so do not cite that figure from it.)
Bears on: T3, the governance history of G15 and WP:LLM, and the later 2026 RfC supersedes the "one-sentence" state it describes.

## D. Commentary and framing

**19. Henner, C. (2026). "Wikipedia at 25: A Wake-Up Call." Wikipedia Signpost, Special report, 15 Jan 2026. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Wikipedia_Signpost/2026-01-15/Special_report (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: news-commentary (opinion essay by a former WMF board chair). Verification: opened; author and date as shown.
Annotation: Claims internet users grew 83% in 2016-2025 (3.3B to 6.0B) while Wikipedia pageviews fell 9% (194B to 177B a year); new registrations fell 36% (317K a month to 202K) while edits per new user rose from 49 to 105.7; one AI developer's crawl-to-referral ratio "nearly 50,000:1"; AI chatbot click-through 0.33% vs Google 8.6%. Argues AI firms train on Wikipedia without reciprocal benefit.
Bears on: T2 and T3. Useful framing but the figures are the author's own compilation; I did not trace them to primary datasets. Low-to-medium trust for numbers.

**20. "Predictions of the end of Wikipedia." English Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictions_of_the_end_of_Wikipedia (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: encyclopedia article (secondary, editable). Verification: opened.
Annotation: Documents repeated forecasts of collapse since early years. Notes English Wikipedia editors with over 100 monthly edits peaked around 2007 at about 50,000, fell to 30,000 by 2014, and cites a 2014 Economist line that editors "fell by a third in seven years". Its AI section reports pageviews down about 8% between 2024 and 2025 and ChatGPT's estimated monthly web traffic overtaking Wikipedia's since May 2025.
Bears on: COMPLICATES T3 by supplying a base rate of failed decline predictions. The "ChatGPT overtook Wikipedia" claim is a third-party estimate; use only with its original source.

## E. LLM training corpora and citations

**21. Touvron, H. et al. (2023). "LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models." arXiv:2302.13971, Feb 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971 (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary technical report (preprint). Verification: read through an arXiv HTML mirror; the PDF itself could not be parsed here.
Annotation: Pre-training mix: Wikipedia 4.5% of sampling proportion, 2.45 epochs, 83 GB; against English CommonCrawl 67.0%, C4 15.0%, Books 4.5%, GitHub 4.5%, ArXiv 2.5%, Stack Exchange 2.0%; about 1.4 trillion tokens in total.
Bears on: T3 premise that Wikipedia is central. It is upweighted (repeated 2.45 times) but a small slice by volume.

**22. Soldaini, L. et al. (2024). "Dolma: an Open Corpus of Three Trillion Tokens for Language Model Pretraining Research." arXiv:2402.00159v2 (June 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00159 (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary technical report (preprint; ACL 2024 venue not checked). Verification: opened arXiv HTML.
Annotation: Table 1 "encyclopedic" subset (Wikipedia and Wikibooks): 16.2 GB, 6.2 million documents, 3.7 billion words, 4.3 billion Llama tokens. That is about 0.14% of the roughly 3,059 billion tokens (my arithmetic from the table figures).
Bears on: COMPLICATES T3. In raw token share Wikipedia is tiny; its importance is quality and curation, not volume. Pair with source 21: share ranges from about 0.14% (tokens, Dolma) to 4.5% (sampling weight, LLaMA).

**23. Punturo, B. (2026). "How ChatGPT sources the web." Profound blog, 3 Feb 2026. https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-citation-sources (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: company research blog (vendor data; medium-low trust, not peer-reviewed). Verification: opened; author and date as shown.
Annotation: Dataset of about 730,000 U.S. English ChatGPT.com conversations, October-December 2025. Wikipedia accounts for 5% of all citations and appears in about 18% of conversations that include citations ("nearly 1 in 6"). Calls Wikipedia the "de facto knowledge layer" for baseline facts but notes it does not dominate citations.
Bears on: T3. Wikipedia is cited often but is not the majority source. Other vendors' figures (7.8%, 26-48% of top-10 citations) appeared only in aggregator summaries: AGGREGATOR ONLY, excluded.

## F. Reddit and Stack Overflow primary data

**24. Reddit, Inc. (2026). "Reddit Reports Fourth Quarter and Full Year 2025 Results." Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1, 5 Feb 2026. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1713445/000171344526000020/earningspressreleaseq425.htm (accessed 3 Oct 2026); and Reddit, Inc. Form 10-K for FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1713445/000171344526000022/rddt-20251231.htm (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary filing. Verification: both opened on sec.gov.
Annotation: Full-year 2025 revenue $2.2 billion (up 69%); full-year other revenue (which includes content licensing) $140 million (up 22%); Q4 other revenue $36 million (up 8%); Q4 DAUq 121.4 million (up 19%); logged-in DAUq 50.7 million per the press release. The 10-K confirms 121.4 million DAUq and says Reddit continues to explore content licensing agreements; it does not state Google or OpenAI deal values in the excerpt I read.
Bears on: T2 by contrast. Reddit is monetising licensing (other revenue about 6% of revenue) while its audience grows. The oft-quoted "$60M Google / about $70M OpenAI, 10% of revenue" appeared only in Slashdot and similar aggregator coverage: AGGREGATOR ONLY. Also, the figure 47 million logged-in DAU found in an aggregator conflicts with the 50.7 million in the press release; use the filing.

**25. Stack Exchange API v2.3, question counts for Stack Overflow, queried by me on 3 Oct 2026. https://api.stackexchange.com/2.3/questions?site=stackoverflow&filter=total with fromdate/todate (accessed 3 Oct 2026)**
Type: primary data (Stack Exchange's own API). Verification: calls returned JSON totals for the windows below. Note the API counts questions currently visible, so deleted questions are excluded and totals may differ slightly from the Data Explorer figures cited by blogs.
Annotation, monthly new questions: Mar 2014: 207,171; Oct 2022: 105,745; Nov 2022: 109,120; Oct 2023: 52,337; Oct 2024: 23,163; Oct 2025: 5,262; Dec 2025: 4,174; Jul 2026: 1,179.
Bears on: T2 and the "Wikipedia is not alone" argument. Volume fell about 52% from Oct 2022 to Oct 2023 and about 95% by Oct 2025. The decline started well before ChatGPT (peak in 2014), so some of it is not LLM-driven. Blog figures (e.g. 1,304 or 1,442 questions in July 2026; 3,862 or 3,312 in Dec 2025) disagree with each other and with this pull: AGGREGATOR ONLY, treat as low trust.

## Gaps and disputes

I could not obtain a primary figure for English Wikipedia active editors by year; the WMF API editor route failed and the editor counts quoted (about 250,000 vs 273,000) differ by definition. Stack Overflow blog figures disagree with each other and with the API pull. Reddit licensing values (Google, OpenAI) are only in secondary coverage. Wikipedia's citation share varies widely by vendor and method. The 8% fall may be partly a bot-classification artefact. The CNBC piece on the January 2026 deals returned HTTP 403 and was excluded. No peer-reviewed study of the post-October 2025 decline was found.
