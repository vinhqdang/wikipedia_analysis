# 9. What editors think of AI developers using their work: search of 5 October 2026

Search question: is there research on how Wikipedia editors feel about AI firms using, and profiting from, their contributions. Result: no systematic study found. Indirect evidence and work in progress follow, with reading status.

| Source | What it offers | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Vetter, Jiang & McDowell (2025), *AI & Society* 40(6), 4309-4322 | Problem-centred interviews with 6 experts (machine learning, Wikimedia, data science, journalism), July 2024 to February 2025. Expert E2: "The ethical problem that I hear about most frequently from Wikipedians is that the situation doesn't seem fundamentally fair. The editors produce this content without compensation, it is openly licensed, and then these big tech companies make so much money from LLMs". E4 (paraphrased): users happy to give content to humans are not necessarily happy to have it fed to a model | Read through the project's Meta-Wiki summary and the Crossref abstract; the Springer page was paywalled. Quotes are second-hand (experts reporting what editors say) and should be checked against the article |
| Bhattacharya (2026), *Rest of World*, 5 February | Three named editors (Telugu, Malayalam, English): one wants "the 'brain' of the internet" trained on quality content; one fears AI could widen the gap between English and regional languages; one stresses finding and fixing knowledge gaps. None of those named reported payment or formal credit | Page read. Journalism, three people, not a sample |
| Gelauff (2026), Meta-Wiki project "Wikipedia contributor outlooks to the future" | Volunteer-led interviews already done in English, Italian, Bangla, Malayalam, Swedish and Telugu communities, with fears of Wikipedia becoming an "invisible backend" for AI systems; a banner survey of logged-in editors planned, with launch scheduled for 5 September 2026 | Project page read on 5 October 2026: draft, empty results section; whether the survey launched was not confirmed. The most promising source to watch or contact |
| Signpost op-ed by Sadads, 21 June 2026 | One editor's view: AI reliance on Wikipedia is an opportunity to influence models; motivation is curiosity and belonging; does not address pay, consent or credit | Summary of the page only |
| Zhou, Cho & Terveen (2026) | Editors' use of LLMs in editing, not their view of AI firms reusing content | Already cited elsewhere in the essay |
| Community Insights 2024 report | Large contributor survey (2,629 completes, 11.9% cumulative response) on motivations for contributing, but no questions on AI. The motivation items could show whether recognition or reciprocity matters to editors | Page read; not mined |
| Wikimedia Research Fund 2025 project "Navigating Today, Shaping Tomorrow: Studying the Role of LLMs on Wikipedia" | Surveys and experiments on editors' use of LLM tools; about use inside Wikipedia, not reuse of its content | Search summary only; OpenReview page blocked |

Reading for the essay: editors' views on AI reuse are not established. The expert-reported unfairness concern fits the fair-play framing of Section 3.3, the named-editor interviews point the other way (pride, interest in model quality), and neither is a sample. The gap stands as stated in Section 6.
