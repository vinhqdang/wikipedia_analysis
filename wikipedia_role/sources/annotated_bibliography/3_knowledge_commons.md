# Lit search 3: Knowledge commons, enclosure, peer production

Verification note: several publisher sites (Nature, Sage, MIT Press, JSTOR, Reuters) returned 403/redirects to the fetch tool, so bibliographic details were confirmed through Crossref API records, arXiv records, Duke repository, Yale LJ site, Penguin Random House, Google Books. Annotations draw only on abstracts/summaries unless stated; no full texts were read.

## A. Foundations (commons, peer production, enclosure)

### 1. Hess & Ostrom (2007)
- Citation: Hess, C., & Ostrom, E. (Eds.). (2007). *Understanding knowledge as a commons: From theory to practice*. MIT Press. (Penguin Random House lists a 2011 paperback; original hardcover 2007.) https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/655188/understanding-knowledge-as-a-commons-by-edited-by-charlotte-hess-and-elinor-ostrom/
- Type: book (edited volume).
- Verification: Penguin Random House catalogue page (title, editors, MIT Press, description); a web search result also listed MIT Press 2007 hardcover, 367 pp.
- Annotation: Treats knowledge as a shared resource and complex ecosystem subject to social dilemmas, applying Ostrom's commons framework (a social-ecological system) rather than treating information as a pure public good. Covers the study, protection (against enclosure via IP, overpatenting, overpricing, lack of preservation) and production of knowledge commons, with library and open-access cases. Seen via catalogue description only.
- Bearing: Supplies the vocabulary for T2: a commons is sustained by community rules and norms, not by the licence alone, so licence-compliant extraction can still violate the commons' social contract. Complicates: Ostrom's framework is about governable communities with boundaries; Wikipedia is open-access by design.
- Keywords: knowledge commons, Ostrom, social dilemmas, enclosure, governance.

### 2. Benkler (2002)
- Citation: Benkler, Y. (2002). Coase's penguin, or, Linux and *The Nature of the Firm*. *Yale Law Journal, 112*(3), 369-446. https://www.yalelawjournal.org/article/coases-penguin-or-linux-and-the-nature-of-the-firm
- Type: peer-reviewed article (law review; edited, not peer-reviewed in the strict sense).
- Verification: Yale Law Journal article page (title, author, vol. 112, Dec 2002). Page range and issue are from my background knowledge, not the page; treat as unconfirmed.
- Annotation: Page summary says it examines Linux/open-source development through Coase's theory of the firm, presenting collaborative decentralised production as an alternative to markets and hierarchies. Only the page summary was seen. (Benkler's argument that "commons-based peer production" rests on intrinsic and social motivation is from the well-known text, not from what I opened.)
- Bearing: Grounds T2: if peer production runs on social-psychological rewards, extraction without recognition or reciprocity may erode the motivational base. Complicates T3: Benkler stresses a modular, low-cost architecture that could also be used by AI firms as free input.
- Keywords: commons-based peer production, Linux, firm, motivation.

### 3. Boyle (2003)
- Citation: Boyle, J. (2003). The second enclosure movement and the construction of the public domain. *Law and Contemporary Problems, 66*(1/2), 33-74. https://scholarship.law.duke.edu/lcp/vol66/iss1/2/
- Type: peer-reviewed article (law review symposium).
- Verification: Duke Law Scholarship Repository record (title, author, vol. 66 no. 1, Winter 2003, pp. 33-74); abstract not displayed.
- Annotation: Abstract not seen. Known from title and its standing: argues that expanding IP over information resembles the English enclosure of common land and that the public domain must be deliberately constructed as a concept. I did not read the text, so details are not confirmed here.
- Bearing: Frames T2 inversely: Boyle's enclosure is private property over commons, while here the concern is reuse of an open commons with no property claims, a different direction of harm (appropriation by extraction rather than enclosure by fencing). Useful as a contrast.
- Keywords: enclosure, public domain, intellectual property.

### 4. Lessig (2004)
- Citation: Lessig, L. (2004). *Free culture: How big media uses technology and the law to lock down culture and control creativity*. Penguin Press.
- Type: book.
- Verification: Google Books record (title, author, Penguin Press, 2004) and the Wikipedia article for ISBN 978-1-59420-006-9.
- Annotation: From the book description: argues that copyright expansion shifts the US from a "free culture" to a "permission culture" and shrinks the public domain, to the benefit of incumbents. Description only.
- Bearing: Lessig's policy aim is more openness, so strong attribution or permission demands against AI reuse sit awkwardly with his position. Complicates T2: a "permission culture" argument could be turned against contributor-protective restrictions on AI training.
- Keywords: free culture, copyright, permission culture, Creative Commons.

## B. Generative AI and the digital commons

### 5. Huang & Siddarth (2023), preprint
- Citation: Huang, S., & Siddarth, D. (2023). *Generative AI and the digital commons* [Preprint]. arXiv:2303.11074. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11074
- Type: preprint.
- Verification: arXiv abstract page (authors, submitted 20 Mar 2023, cs.CY).
- Annotation: Argues foundation models trained on public data may harm the digital commons while not compensating contributors, and that privacy and copyright approaches are inadequate. Proposes transparency, funded auditing consortia, an expectation that AI firms contribute quality data back to the commons, and shared ownership of fine-tuning data. Abstract only.
- Bearing: Closest match to T2: licence compliance versus reciprocity. The "contribute back" expectation is the social-contract claim in practical form.
- Keywords: digital commons, foundation models, reciprocity, governance.

### 6. Noroozian et al. (2025), preprint
- Citation: Noroozian, A., Aldana, L., Arisi, M., et al. (2025). *Generative AI and the future of the digital commons: Five open questions and knowledge gaps* [Preprint]. arXiv:2508.06470. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06470
- Type: preprint (24 authors).
- Verification: arXiv abstract page; skimmed PDF through a summariser (it mentions CC BY-SA and preference signals).
- Annotation: Names five concerns: contribution decline, web closure (sites restricting crawlers), standards gap, content integrity (synthetic pollution) and cost distribution. Frames the commons as "commoning" practice, not just content, and advocates an "AI commons". Read via summary, not in full.
- Bearing: Supports T2 and T3 and gives a map of open empirical questions. It is a research agenda, not evidence.
- Keywords: digital commons, commoning, web closure, synthetic content.

### 7. Longpre et al. (2024), preprint
- Citation: Longpre, S., Mahari, R., Lee, A., Lund, C., et al. (2024). *Consent in crisis: The rapid decline of the AI data commons* [Preprint]. arXiv:2407.14933. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14933
- Type: preprint.
- Verification: arXiv abstract page (authors, 20 Jul 2024).
- Annotation: Audits 14,000 web domains in C4, RefinedWeb and Dolma and finds a sharp rise in restrictions in 2023-24: about 5% of C4 tokens fully restricted, 45% restricted by Terms of Service. Finds mismatches between ToS and robots.txt. Warns that this threatens data diversity and scaling. Abstract only.
- Bearing: Direct empirical support for the enclosure reading and for T3 (open web closing); also shows the enclosure is by data holders reacting to AI, not by AI firms.
- Keywords: data commons, robots.txt, consent, web restrictions.

## C. Empirical effects on contributions and engagement

### 8. del Rio-Chanona, Laurentsyeva & Wachs (2024)
- Citation: del Rio-Chanona, M., Laurentsyeva, N., & Wachs, J. (2024). Large language models reduce public knowledge sharing on online Q&A platforms. *PNAS Nexus, 3*(9). https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae400 (preprint version: arXiv:2307.07367, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07367, titled "Are large language models a threat to digital public goods? Evidence from activity on Stack Overflow")
- Type: peer-reviewed article (preprint also exists).
- Verification: Crossref record for the DOI (title, authors, PNAS Nexus vol. 3, 2024); arXiv record for the preprint abstract.
- Annotation: Difference-in-differences comparing Stack Overflow with platforms where ChatGPT has limited access finds about a 16% fall in weekly posts, growing over time and concentrated in widely used languages. Post-ChatGPT posts get similar vote scores, so the loss is not just low-quality duplicates. Authors warn this threatens the open data that future models need. Abstract only.
- Bearing: Core evidence for T3 (Stack Overflow's collapse) and the free-rider dynamic. Complicates it: the control group is Russian/Chinese platforms and the design identifies a decline, not a "collapse".
- Keywords: Stack Overflow, ChatGPT, substitution, digital public goods.

### 9. Burtch, Lee & Chen (2024)
- Citation: Burtch, G., Lee, D., & Chen, Z. (2024). The consequences of generative AI for online knowledge communities. *Scientific Reports, 14*, Article 10413. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-61221-0
- Type: peer-reviewed article.
- Verification: Crossref API record (title, authors, journal, vol. 14, article 10413, 2024).
- Annotation: Stack Overflow and Reddit data (Oct 2021-Mar 2023): significant declines in visits and question volume at Stack Overflow, especially on topics where ChatGPT performs well; Reddit communities show no decline, which the authors suggest reflects social connection resisting disengagement. Loss concentrated among newer users. Abstract only.
- Bearing: Supports T3 for Stack Overflow, but the Reddit result complicates "social platforms are collapsing" and hints that socially embedded communities (like Wikipedia's) resist.
- Keywords: online communities, ChatGPT, newcomers, social ties.

### 10. Lyu et al. (2025), conference paper
- Citation: Lyu, L., Siderius, J., Li, H., Acemoglu, D., Huttenlocher, D., & Ozdaglar, A. (2025). Wikipedia contributions in the wake of ChatGPT. In *Companion Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference 2025*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3701716.3715543 (preprint: arXiv:2503.00757)
- Type: peer-reviewed conference paper (the DOI was surfaced by search and the arXiv page gives WWW 2025; I did not open the ACM page).
- Verification: arXiv abstract page (authors, 2 Mar 2025, WWW 2025).
- Annotation: Difference-in-differences on Wikipedia: new, popular articles whose content overlaps with ChatGPT's output saw larger declines in editing and viewership after Nov 2022 than dissimilar ones ("heterogeneous substitution effects"). Summary only.
- Bearing: Directly relevant to Wikipedia: contributors and readers lost where AI substitutes. Complicates by showing the effect is partial, not general.
- Keywords: Wikipedia, ChatGPT, substitution, difference-in-differences.

### 11. Reeves, Yin & Simperl (2025)
- Citation: Reeves, N., Yin, W., & Simperl, E. (2025). Exploring the impact of ChatGPT on Wikipedia engagement. *Collective Intelligence, 4*(3). https://doi.org/10.1177/26339137251372599
- Type: peer-reviewed article.
- Verification: Crossref API record (title, authors, Collective Intelligence vol. 4 issue 3, 2025); Sage page itself returned 403.
- Annotation: A 36-month study across 12 language editions finds "no evidence of an overall decline" in page views, visitors, edits or editors; both views and visitors grew. Growth was lower where ChatGPT was available than where it was not. Abstract only.
- Bearing: COMPLICATES T3/T2. The harm to Wikipedia is slower growth, not collapse, which conflicts with the Lyu et al. headline when read alone.
- Keywords: Wikipedia, engagement, ChatGPT, language editions.

## D. Model collapse and data dependence

### 12. Shumailov et al. (2024)
- Citation: Shumailov, I., Shumaylov, Z., Zhao, Y., Papernot, N., Anderson, R., & Gal, Y. (2024). AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data. *Nature, 631*(8022), 755-759. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07566-y
- Type: peer-reviewed article.
- Verification: Crossref API record (authors, title, Nature 631(8022), 755-759, 2024); nature.com blocked.
- Annotation: Training on model-generated content causes irreversible degradation ("model collapse") in which tails of the original distribution vanish, shown for LLMs, VAEs and Gaussian mixtures. Notes that human-generated data grow in value as AI text proliferates. Abstract only.
- Bearing: Basis for the argument that AI firms depend on human commons such as Wikipedia, strengthening the reciprocity claim in T2.
- Keywords: model collapse, synthetic data, human data.

### 13. Gerstgrasser et al. (2024), preprint
- Citation: Gerstgrasser, M., Schaeffer, R., Dey, A., Rafailov, R., Sleight, H., Hughes, J., ... Koyejo, S. (2024). *Is model collapse inevitable? Breaking the curse of recursion by accumulating real and synthetic data* [Preprint]. arXiv:2404.01413. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413
- Type: preprint.
- Verification: arXiv abstract page (authors, submitted 1 Apr 2024).
- Annotation: Shows empirically and in a linear-model theory that if real data are retained and synthetic data accumulate rather than replace them, test error stays bounded and collapse is avoided. Abstract only.
- Bearing: COMPLICATES the dependency/leverage argument: collapse depends on practice, and what matters is keeping old human data, not a continuing supply of new contributions.
- Keywords: model collapse, data accumulation.

### 14. Villalobos et al. (2024), preprint
- Citation: Villalobos, P., Ho, A., Sevilla, J., Besiroglu, T., Heim, L., & Hobbhahn, M. (2024). *Will we run out of data? Limits of LLM scaling based on human-generated data* [Preprint, rev. 4 Jun 2024]. arXiv:2211.04325. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04325
- Type: preprint (later presented at ICML 2024; I did not confirm that).
- Verification: arXiv abstract page (authors, v1 Oct 2022, revised Jun 2024).
- Annotation: Forecasts that training sets will roughly match the stock of public human text between 2026 and 2032 if trends continue; suggests synthetic data, transfer learning and data efficiency may allow progress beyond. Abstract only.
- Bearing: The "data scarcity" premise for T2: freshly written, curated human text becomes strategic. Complicates it by noting escape routes.
- Keywords: data scarcity, scaling, human text.

## E. Commodification: licensing deals

### 15. Chintalapoodi (2024), law-firm commentary on Reddit-Google
- Citation: Chintalapoodi, P. (2024, March 15). Reddit and Google enter into AI content licensing agreement. Chip Law Group. https://www.chiplawgroup.com/reddit-and-google-enter-into-ai-content-licensing-agreement/
- Type: news-commentary.
- Verification: opened the page (author, date, content). Reuters original could not be opened.
- Annotation: Reports a roughly $60 million per year deal for Google to use Reddit user content for AI training, with $203 million in projected AI-licensing revenue over three years per SEC filings. Notes Reddit's terms give it broad rights over user posts while some users object, and cites an expert warning of backlash.
- Bearing: Concrete case of user-made commons sold by the platform without user compensation (T3 and T2). Secondary source; a Reuters or S-1 citation would be better.
- Keywords: Reddit, data licensing, user-generated content.

### 16. Stack Overflow (2024), company press release
- Citation: Stack Overflow. (2024, May 6). *Stack Overflow and OpenAI partnership*. https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/openai-partnership
- Type: news-commentary (corporate press release; primary source).
- Verification: opened the Stack Overflow press page.
- Annotation: OpenAI gets access to Stack Overflow content via OverflowAPI; OpenAI commits to provide attribution to the community within ChatGPT; Stack Overflow will "reinvest in community-driven features". It is a PR statement of the company's side only.
- Bearing: Shows an attribution/reinvestment promise as the commercial answer to the reciprocity worry (T2); useful to test whether Wikipedia gets comparable terms. Note the content is CC BY-SA licensed, and community members disputed the charging for dumps (from a secondary news search, not verified here).
- Keywords: Stack Overflow, OpenAI, attribution, OverflowAPI.

### 17. Becker (2026), Wikimedia Foundation
- Citation: Becker, L. (2026, July 16). The cost of "free": How Wikimedia Enterprise protects Wikipedia in the AI era. Wikimedia Foundation. https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/07/16/wikimedia-enterprise-protecting-wikipedia-ai/
- Type: news-commentary (organisation blog; primary source).
- Verification: opened the page.
- Annotation: Argues bots now exceed human traffic and strain infrastructure; content stays CC BY-SA 4.0 free, but commercial reusers should help "carry the burden of sustaining those commons" via paid Enterprise access. Says Enterprise has repaid its initial investment and caps commercial revenue at 30% of annual funding; public APIs are rate-limited to push heavy users to paid access.
- Bearing: Evidence that Wikimedia itself treats licence-compliant reuse as insufficient and seeks reciprocity (T2), and that the commons strategy is partly commercial. Interested source.
- Keywords: Wikimedia Enterprise, CC BY-SA, crawlers, sustainability.

## F. Dissenting or complicating view

### 18. Kallel & El Louadi (2026), preprint
- Citation: Kallel, M., & El Louadi, M. (2026). *Cognitive commons in the age of generative intelligence: A heterodox appraisal of the knowledge erosion hypothesis* [Preprint]. arXiv:2607.13272. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13272
- Type: preprint (12-page work in progress).
- Verification: arXiv abstract page (submitted 14 Jul 2026).
- Annotation: Argues against inevitable-collapse narratives; frames the policy problem as designing polycentric stewardship that keeps the commons robust to heterogeneous human responses, drawing on cognitive ergonomics, institutional ecology and novice expertise. Abstract only; a low-visibility preprint.
- Bearing: Complicates the enclosure reading, but is about cognition and learning more than data commons, so weak support. I found no strong peer-reviewed text that argues AI is good for the commons.
- Keywords: cognitive commons, polycentric governance, knowledge erosion.

## Gaps and disputes
Evidence on Wikipedia is mixed: Lyu et al. find substitution on AI-overlapping articles, while Reeves et al. find overall growth, lower where ChatGPT is available. Stack Overflow is the clearest collapse case, but Burtch et al. show Reddit did not fall, so "platforms are collapsing" overgeneralises. Collapse theory is contested (Shumailov versus Gerstgrasser), which weakens a dependency argument. I found no peer-reviewed work testing the T2 "social contract" claim for Wikipedia, and no strong peer-reviewed pro-AI-commons paper. Items 15-17 are interested or secondary sources. Boyle, Benkler and Lessig were only seen through metadata and descriptions.
