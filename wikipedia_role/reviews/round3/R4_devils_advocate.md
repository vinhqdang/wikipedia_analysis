## Devil's Advocate Review (R4)

### Calibration Status
`NOT_CALIBRATED`

Genuine strengths (brief): unusually candid about evidence limits (Section 4.2, closing "three limits"); the concession that no breach is claimed is honest; Section 6.1 gives one concrete, checkable proposal.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The essay's central move is to redescribe a dispute about what is owed as a fact about norm dynamics, and then to treat the redescription as an answer. Bicchieri-style norms and Lewis conventions are descriptive devices: they explain why people behave and feel as they do. They are silent on whether a given expectation is justified. So "the feeling of betrayal registers lapsing expectations" is true of any grievance whatever: the expectations of a displaced tenant, a deskilled worker or an unpaid author also "lapse" when conditions change. The account would be equally satisfied if reusers owed contributors a great deal (a labour-based or rights-based view: unpaid, attributed labour was extracted under a licence nobody could foresee being applied to training) or nothing. An account compatible with every normative outcome explains none of them.

The three-stance structure then does the real work, and it is rigged. The "eternalist" is built from fair play, natural duty and beneficence, each dispatched in a paragraph via encyclopedia summaries. The strongest rivals are absent: a moral-realist reciprocity view that does not need joining or indispensability (the beneficiary of a gift economy owes return as a matter of gratitude or non-exploitation), a labour/desert view, and a public-benefit view in which AI access to a commons is the commons' purpose, so nothing is owed because nothing is lost. Having rejected two caricatures, the essay lands on a "conventional, defeasible reason", which is weak enough that nobody must act on it. The middle way is where a reader arrives when the extremes are defined to be unattractive. The Buddhist lens does no work the Bicchieri/Lewis material has not already done; the essay concedes this ("If those distinctions could be drawn without the vocabulary... the essay would stand"), which is the definition of ornament. Finally, the operative conclusion, carry the dispute record with the text, does not follow from the norm theory at all. It follows from a premise about epistemic function that is asserted, not shown, and its payoff (readers audit disputes in generated answers) is untested. Meanwhile the pessimistic rival (AI absorbs the function regardless, and the community that supplies the dispute record is the thing eroding) is acknowledged only as an uncertainty.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. Labour/rights view: contributors gave attributed work into a licence regime whose scope could not anticipate training; the licence silence is itself the claim of injustice, so "no breach" is the wrong test. A claim-right to attribution or compensation (or collective bargaining, data trusts, levies) is a rival path, mentioned only as "other routes".
2. Moral-realist reciprocity: gratitude/non-exploitation ethics does not need joining or indispensability; the essay's "failure at a premise" argument does not touch it.
3. Public-benefit view: open knowledge feeding widely used tools is the stated aim of free licences; readership decline may be a transfer of access, not a loss.
4. Pessimistic absorption: AI retrieval and synthetic corpora replace curation; the dispute record is carried by no one because no one reads it. The essay's 6.1 may be a function that cannot be sustained without readers and editors.
5. Simpler explanation of the "betrayal" feeling: ordinary status/credit loss and fear of replacement, with no norm-complex needed.
6. Platform-level explanation of Wikipedia's editor decline (Halfaker) predating LLMs, which suggests LLM reuse is not the main cause of the conditions' change.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Editors themselves (no survey; the essay relies on proxies).
- Readers who use generated answers (no evidence on whether they need dispute records).
- AI developers and open-weight/non-commercial users (the "reusers" are treated as a single class).
- Non-English communities beyond a one-edition appendix.
- Knowledge holders outside Wikipedia's source regime (mentioned in 6.3 but not consulted).
- Legal and policy actors (regulators, collective rights bodies).

### Unexamined Premise
That the function Wikipedia serves is something that could be separated from its form (the community, its labour and its readers) and carried elsewhere. If auditability exists only in the live practice of answerability, a "dispute record" that travels as data preserves the artefact and not the function, and "hold the function, release the form" is a hope, not an argument.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The essay is more candid than most about its limits, which makes the overreach in the framing more visible than the evidence gaps.
- A falsifiability section is the single most useful addition: state what observations would count against the lapsing-norm reading (for example, editors reporting a firm, justified expectation of return, with majority endorsement).
- The thinness of the claim in Section 5.4 could be an asset if the essay committed to a testable proposal (a survey design or a pilot of dispute-record export).
