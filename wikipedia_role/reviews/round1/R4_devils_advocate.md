## Devil's Advocate Review

Manuscript: "The last bastion is a community: Wikipedia in the era of language models" (essay_full_draft_v3.md). Blind review; no other project file was read. No external sources were fetched; points that rest on outside knowledge are flagged "verify".

Genuine strengths, briefly: the essay states its own limits unusually candidly (it disclaims illegality, betrayal and large harm), treats the licence question with care, and keeps the "gift" objection in view. The attack below targets what it still leaves standing.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The essay fails at its hinge. It says Wikipedia's worth is auditability, and that licence-compliant reuse by model builders creates a light duty of upkeep grounded in fair play, natural duty and beneficence. But each ground is conceded to fail exactly where it would bind developers. Fair play: the restrictions "fall on editors and not on them", so developers have not joined the scheme. Natural duty: it is "an argument by analogy" from just states to a voluntary association. Beneficence: "the harm is mild", so the principle "does not clearly apply". Three arguments that each lose a different premise do not add up to a duty. They add up to a preference, which the Foundation already voices.

What remains is the gift view. Contributors chose an irrevocable, royalty-free licence so that nobody would owe anything, and a commons is meant to be used. The rival/non-rival split rescues the duty only by relocating it to a "practice" that cannot claim anything and whose carriers, by the essay's admission, have not been asked what they expect.

The factual antecedents lean on the Foundation's own reports: that almost all models train on Wikipedia (0.14% of tokens in one corpus), that traffic fell (independent studies disagree), and that models depend on this practice rather than on substitutable human text. The auditability thesis is also thin. Retrieval systems that cite already deliver provenance, readers rarely audit, and contestability is exercised by few people, and in some editions has been captured.

Finally, if Wikipedia must reform for its claim to be strong, the claim is presently unestablished. If the reforms stand "whether or not reusers help", they are not derived from the argument at all. Either way the conclusion is: do something, in forms of your choosing, that the Foundation already asks for.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **Gift view.** Contributors elected a royalty-free, irrevocable licence; the licence and the Foundation's "free forever" language are the commitment. Anything further is charity by recipients, and the essay's "light duty" collapses into this.
2. **Commons-meant-to-be-used view.** A commons that conditions access on reciprocity is no longer the commons the movement defends. Longpre et al. (cited) show closure as the cost of conditionality; the essay's soft signals are not shown to avoid it.
3. **Public-benefit view of AI.** If models deliver Wikipedia's knowledge to readers who would never visit (notably in lower-resource languages), the mission of "free knowledge for every human" is advanced, and any upkeep duty is outweighed or satisfied by the benefit. The essay's "no reader harm" concession is not weighed as a benefit.
4. **Retrieval-with-citation already preserves auditability.** Provenance can be delivered at answer level, with links back to the source. The only residual claim, contestability through the recorded process, is exercised by few readers. The essay does not test whether citation links are followed, nor whether answer-level provenance is sufficient.
5. **Substitutability.** The dependence that grounds the duty may be on human-written text in general, not on this practice. Alternatives (curated corpora, licensed news, books) could replace it.
6. **Decline explained elsewhere.** Editor and reader trends (the post-2007 editor plateau, mobile, search-engine changes, social media) predate and are largely independent of LLMs, so strain attributable to reuse is smaller than implied.
7. **Existing reciprocity.** Enterprise partnerships, search-engine referral, infrastructure and tooling funded by large firms may already constitute a "light" return. The essay lacks the terms and thus cannot say the duty is unmet.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Editors and administrators of the practice (unmeasured, though they are "the people who carry it"); especially non-English communities.
- AI developers by size and type: open-source, academic and non-profit builders (e.g. the Dolma corpus authors, the essay's own source) for whom a "pooled arrangement" may be a barrier.
- Readers in lower-resource languages who receive Wikipedia-derived knowledge through models.
- Authors and publishers of sources Wikipedia cites (the upstream reliance chain).
- Search and retrieval providers who mediate most traffic and set display practices.
- Regulators and rights bodies (opt-out regimes, code-of-practice frameworks) that could do the work the essay assigns to norms.
- The Foundation's Enterprise unit and its customers as a party with interests in both the "duty" and the "terms".

### Unexamined Premise
The essay presumes that the object of obligation is a "practice" distinct from, and more morally salient than, the text and its contributors, and that this practice is a good the reusers can be said to "rely on". Everything in §3.3 and §4 depends on moving the claim from persons (who gave freely) to an institution (which cannot give or withhold consent). That premise is never defended.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The essay's honest concession list (§1, §6) pre-empts several attacks, but the concessions are not carried into the strength of the abstract's conclusion ("sustaining it calls for...").
- The sharpest internal tension (C1) could be repaired by choosing one horn explicitly: either treat Section 5 as an independent argument about Wikipedia's own legitimacy, or specify a threshold of fairness at which the reuser claim switches on.
- A short empirical test would materially strengthen the position, for example whether answer-level citations to Wikipedia produce click-through, or whether Enterprise terms already deliver credit and route back.
