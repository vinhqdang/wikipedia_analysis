## 2. Verification of the new primary-source quotations

**Hume, Treatise III.ii.ii (Gutenberg #4705).** I checked the quotations against the Gutenberg plain text.
- "justice derives its origin" and "only from the selfishness and confined generosity of men, along with the scanty provision nature has made for his wants" are accurate and are in III.ii.ii. The essay splits them into two fragments in reverse order (Hume: "it is only from ... that justice derives its origin"). The fragments are verbatim, so this is acceptable, but the splice should be signalled or the sentence quoted whole.
- "only a general sense of common interest; which sense all the members of the society express to one another" is accurate and is in III.ii.ii.
- **Misattribution.** "arises artificially, though necessarily from education, and human conventions" is verbatim, but it is the closing sentence of III.ii.i ("Justice, whether a natural or artificial virtue?"). It is not in III.ii.ii, yet the single citation "book III, part II, section II" covers all three quotations. The essay's text drops "and injustice" outside the quotation marks. That is harmless, but the sentence is about the *sense* of justice, not about obligation.
- **Misreading by omission.** The sentence the essay quotes opens "This convention is not of the nature of a promise". Hume goes on to ground compliance in interest, conditional on reciprocity: "it will be for my interest to leave another in the possession of his goods, provided he will act in the same manner with regard to me." This supports the essay's conditionality thesis. It also shows that Hume's convention binds *parties to mutual forbearance*, and that a reuser who takes and gives nothing is the Humean free rider (the "sensible knave" of *Enquiry* IX.ii). It also bears on the Hawley discussion, since Hume denies the convention is a commitment of the promissory kind. The essay uses Hume to show that obligation is conditional but not to show that anyone outside the scheme has a reason. Unaddressed, this cuts against the "weak reason for developers" claim.
- Hume's own circumstances of justice include scarcity, and the passage immediately before the quoted proposition says that with abundance "justice and injustice would be equally unknown". The essay says (§6.3) that Wikipedia's text is non-rival. By Hume's own condition, justice would then have no occasion with respect to the text. The essay's move to the rival "practice" is therefore load-bearing and should be flagged as the essay's response to Hume's condition.

**Rawls, TJ §22, Rev. ed. pp. 109-110.** The two quotations ("the normal conditions under which human cooperation is both possible and necessary"; "unless these circumstances existed there would be no occasion for the virtue of justice") match my knowledge of §22, and the pagination is plausible. I could not retrieve the book text through the available fetch routes, so the page numbers are unverified. The authors should check them against the print edition. The reading, however, is a problem:
- §6.1 and §2.4 present this as "Hume's and Rawls's conventionalism". Rawls is not a conventionalist. He is a constructivist, and he borrows Hume's circumstances (objective and subjective) as the setting for justice as a virtue of *basic institutions*. The quotation shows that justice has an occasion only under certain conditions. It does not show that the norms are conventional.
- The same Rawls is cited in §6.2 for a natural duty of justice that "does not depend on a scheme". In Rawls, the natural duty applies to just institutions that "exist and apply to us". It is conditioned on the same circumstances. The essay cites Rawls for conditionality in §6.1 and then calls his natural duty a ground that "does not depend on a scheme" (see N1).

## 3. New problems introduced by the revision

**N1. The relapse of W1 in the new taxonomy.** *Anchor*: §6.2 heading "Grounds that do not depend on a scheme"; Abstract "unconditional grounds do not clearly apply". Natural duty depends on the existence of just institutions; Singer and Kant depend on need, capacity and cost. "Not scheme-dependent" is defensible; "unconditional" is not. **Fix**: use "scheme-independent" throughout, drop "unconditional" from the Abstract, and say what each ground does condition on.

**N2. Humean convention is listed as a ground and also as "the scheme itself".** *Anchor*: §6.2 "And Hume's convention is the scheme itself." and Abstract "fair play, cooperative beneficence and Humean convention bind only where such a scheme exists". The scheme is the precondition of the grounds, not a further ground. It also gives no reason to someone outside the scheme (see Hume above). **Fix**: treat Hume as the diagnosis of why scheme-dependence arises, and name the ground (interest-in-reciprocity, or fair play) that does the binding.

**N3. Central non sequitur: the scheme-dependent reason is made to ask for the scheme to be built.** *Anchor*: §6.4 "If the reason depends on a scheme, and the scheme may not have formed for developers, building one is the practical form of what the reason asks." By the essay's own account the reason exists only where the scheme does, so it cannot ground a reason to create it. A premise from a scheme-independent ground is needed. Rawls's natural duty has exactly this form, "to further just arrangements not yet established" at small cost (TJ pp. 98-99, cited from memory; check), and the essay has classed that ground as not clearly applicable. **Fix**: either invoke a scheme-independent premise explicitly (cost-limited natural duty or beneficence to establish cooperative arrangements) and reconsider its status in §6.3, or drop the claim that building the scheme is what the reason asks and present it as prudential advice.

**N4. The conventional machinery gives developers nothing, and the final claim does not come from it.** *Anchor*: §5.2 "a demand grounded in a norm that never extended must offer reasons for joining" together with §6.3 "the reason ... is weak where ... the scheme has not formed" and the Abstract "Because these norms are conventional ...". On the essay's own account, if developers lie outside the reference network, the convention-based bridge of §2.1 does not reach them. The reason it leaves them is the fair-play-type premise of §6.3, which is not conventional. The two halves of the essay (norm lapse and obligation) are still not joined. **Fix**: state that the convention-based reason reaches only those inside the reference network, that the reason for developers is a moral principle whose application conditions are partly conventional, and revise the Abstract accordingly.

**N5. Classification without application.** *Anchor*: §6.3 "The result is a classification and not a derivation." The fair-play conditions (restrictions borne, benefits accepted, mutual submission) are never applied to Wikipedia and reusers. The round 3 point that an openly licensed, unrestricted scheme cannot ground fairness claims against takers unless it includes reciprocity (ShareAlike) is not drawn out. §6.3 also says the Hart right "would belong to those who submitted to the restrictions", yet the essay never says what restrictions editors bear in relation to reusers. The conclusion that the reason is "weak and conditional" is therefore asserted, not shown. **Fix**: run the three or four conditions of each scheme-dependent ground against the case and say which fail.

**N6. Overstatement in the conclusion versus §5.2.** *Anchor*: §8 "not yet formed among the new class of reusers" versus §5.2 "For AI developers it is not shown" and §5.5 "None of these is tested here." The Abstract ("may never have formed") is consistent with §5.2; the conclusion is not. **Fix**: use "may not have formed" in §8.

**N7. Title, keywords and the Buddhist material.** *Anchor*: title "impermanence"; keywords; §6.5 "Hume's conventionalism could carry the argument alone". The essay now concedes that the lens carries nothing, yet it keeps the lens in the title and the keywords. §8 says the material is "retained for the structure of the middle position", while §6.5 says the middle position is Humean. **Fix**: either earn it (the two truths and dependent designation, which would link convention to existence) or cut it to a footnote-length remark and retitle ("Conditioned norms ...").

**N8. The function remains the unargued main normative choice, and "answerability" is thin.** *Anchor*: §3 "Together they make a claim accountable in the sense that a reader can trace it, challenge it, and know who answers for it." and Tollefsen "the essay leaves that open". Answerability to whom, with what remedy, when contributors are pseudonymous volunteers with no sanction? This is where the dispute-record proposal in §7.1 depends on a philosophical account. **Fix**: use O'Neill's intelligent accountability and the testimony literature (Coady, Goldberg, Faulkner, Zagzebski) and state what the answerable party owes.

**N9. Strawson-based bridge.** *Anchor*: §2.1 "Strawson's account of reactive attitudes points the same way". Strawson's demand is a demand for goodwill; it presupposes the demandee's membership in the moral community. Whether it supplies the *demandee* with a reason, rather than registering the *demander's* expectation, is exactly what Wallace and others dispute. As cited, the use risks the bridging error it was meant to cure. **Fix**: add Wallace (1994) and state that the demand yields a reason only for those who accept the standing of the demander.

**N10. Minor.** (i) "Wikimedia Foundation (2025e)" appears in the reference list with no matching citation in the text, and the sequence skips c and d. (ii) "Real commitments ... run to platforms" (§5.1) conflicts with the licence's attribution duty (§2.2). (iii) Hume and Rawls page and section locators: apply the Hume correction above. (iv) The Gallert and van der Velden entry still says "unedited preliminary version".

## 4. Is the argument a genuine contribution for Philosophy & Technology?

**What is now sound.** The essay is a well-sourced, honest case study. It has a defensible functional thesis (a citation link carries provenance but not the contest or the answerable community), a specific and testable recommendation (carry the dispute record), a good genetic account of why contributors feel betrayed, a clear lapse versus non-formation distinction (§5.2, with the reference-network point correctly made), and a falsification section (§5.5). The retreat from "binds" to "a weak conditional reason", and the admission that the reason is fair play's, make the claims defensible. For this venue the case-study plus conceptual analysis format is acceptable.

**What it contributes philosophically.** The first thesis (accountability of testimony and the portability of the dispute record) is the contribution. It is modest but real, and it has a policy payoff. The second thesis (what reusers owe) is now a survey and sorting of familiar grounds that ends by conceding the fair-play premise. Its originality is low. It will be acceptable if it is presented as an application and clarification, not as a new account of obligation, and if N3-N5 are repaired. It is not yet a distinct contribution to the philosophy of norms or convention.

**What a specialist would still demand.**
1. An argument, not a quotation, for how mutually held normative expectations give a reason, with Gilbert (joint commitment), Hart's internal point of view, Marmor, and Scanlon (reliance and assurance) engaged. Without it the "conventional reason" is not established.
2. The application of each scheme-dependent ground's conditions to the Wikipedia case (N5), and a scheme-independent premise for the claim that a scheme should be built (N3).
3. An argued choice of function, with alternative functions and their verdicts (W7, N8).
4. The core testimony and social-epistemology literature (Coady, Lackey, Goldberg, Zagzebski, O'Neill) in §3, since the first thesis lives there.
5. A decision about the Buddhist framing: earn it or remove it from the title (N7).
6. Correct citation and reading of Hume and Rawls (section 2 above), plus the Humean knave objection.
7. An objections section that states the real objections (W13).
