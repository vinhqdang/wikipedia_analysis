## Abstract

Wikipedia, once barred from classroom citation, is now invoked as a safeguard against machine-generated falsehood, even as its readership falls and the licence under which firms train models on its text says nothing about return. The essay treats this reversal as a case of how norms depend on conditions. It argues that Wikipedia's authority rests on a complex of credibility, contribution and reciprocity norms whose function is to make testimony accountable: traceable to its sources, open to challenge, and answered for by a community. Because these norms are conventional, they depend on circumstances, several of which have shifted. Some norms appear to be lapsing, some are being rewritten by the community, and for AI developers a reciprocity norm may never have formed. The sense of betrayal voiced by contributors registers unmet expectations, not by itself a broken commitment. Sorting the grounds on which reusers might owe something by their dependence on a cooperative scheme, the essay finds that fair play, cooperative beneficence and Humean convention bind only where such a scheme exists, that Kantian gratitude, the best-fitting unconditional ground, asks little and extends to firms only contestably, and that what is owed is a weak reason whose force depends on whether a scheme can be built. Buddhist impermanence serves as an interpretive resonance. The task is to hold the function and release the form; the most specific proposal is that the record of dispute behind Wikipedia's claims should travel with them into the systems that reuse its text.

**Keywords:** Wikipedia; large language models; social norms; convention; impermanence; accountability
