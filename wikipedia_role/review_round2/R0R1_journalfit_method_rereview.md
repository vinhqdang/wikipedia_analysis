## 5. Remaining weaknesses

### W1: The Teahouse evidence is described as something it is not
**Severity**: Major | **Evidence Anchor**: text: §5.2 "newcomers invited by a host to the Teahouse" and "The first intervention put a named person in touch with each newcomer" | **Confidence**: 4, core competence in reading experimental evidence; verified against the paper.
The paper says invitations "are generally delivered by a robot called HostBot" and signed by a host who agreed to have their name listed. Only 331 of the invited group took part, which the essay notes. So the trial measures the effect of a bot-delivered invitation to a welcoming space. It does not show that "a named person in touch with each newcomer" retained editors, and the essay's own parenthetical ("counts invitation and not participation") contradicts the next sentences. The recommendation "pair rejection with a named human response, as the Teahouse did" builds on this reading. The contrast with the Newcomer Homepage (null on retention) is also drawn across different editions, years and designs. The essay should state that the two results are not comparable, or soften the conclusion. Fix: restate as "an invitation, bot-delivered and signed by a host, to a place where questions are answered by people"; drop "named person" as the mechanism or present it as a hypothesis; keep the recommendation if it is labelled conjecture.

### W2: Section 3.3 is out of proportion and still not tight
**Severity**: Major | **Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "The reason is therefore weak and conditional" | **Confidence**: 4, editorial judgement of fit and balance.
It is 1,585 words, the longest section, to defend a conclusion the author calls weak and conditional on a premise ("strain") that §3.2 says is not established. The "To whom is the reason owed?" paragraph packs Hart, Viljoen, Benkler and Nissenbaum, Zwolinski, Mauss, Cicero, Ennius and Frischmann into one block of about 500 words. Cicero and Mauss are cited to say what the argument does not claim. Round 1 asked for the duty to be demoted; the demotion happened in the claim but not in the space. Fix: reduce §3.3 to about 900 words. State the shared premise and the conditional in numbered steps. Move Cicero, Mauss and the betrayal note to a single sentence each, or cut them. Let Hart and Viljoen carry directedness. Keep the "What the three share" paragraph.

### W3: Philosophical premises are carried by encyclopedia summaries
**Severity**: Minor, with the Klosko point near Major | **Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "(all as reported in Dagger & Lefkowitz, 2021, section 4.2)" | **Confidence**: 4, competence in source handling.
Declaring second-hand status is honest. Klosko's "indispensable" condition carries one of the two stated doubts about fair play ("hardly indispensable ... to firms that could use other data"), and I could not locate the quoted phrase in retrieved text. Mauss is read "in its standard reading". Fix: consult Klosko (1992) and Simmons (1979) directly for the two points actually used, or reduce the claim to what Dagger and Lefkowitz state. Add locators for Fricker, Goldman, Frischmann, Hess and Ostrom and Nozick, or drop the page-specific paraphrases.

### W4: The central absence claim in §5.1 rests on one documentation page
