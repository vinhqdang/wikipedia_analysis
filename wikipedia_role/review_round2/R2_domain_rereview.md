## Recommendation signal

**Minor-to-Major Revision, leaning Major for a philosophy venue, Minor for a policy or AI-and-society venue.** The revision moved the essay a long way. The thesis is now a pro tanto reason, Section 5 is declared independent of it, the claims are hedged, and the literature base is much better. The paper no longer overclaims. The cost is that the duty section now establishes very little, and the grounds it uses fit its own facts poorly (W1-W3 below). A specialist would still demand a coherent account of what remains of fair play once its acceptance condition and its directedness are both disclaimed, and an argument for the commons extension that goes beyond saying the step is "the essay's own".

## Part 2. New problems, introduced or exposed by the revision

### W1: The "shared premise" is partly constructed and its trigger is unmet
**Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4, core expertise in obligation theory
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "What the three share" — "whose use strains the conditions of its upkeep" — and "the strain on which all three depend was not established in Section 3.2."
**Problem**: Fair play (Hart, Rawls) turns on benefit received and burdens borne under a scheme's rules. It does not require that the free rider's use strain the scheme; the harmless-free-rider case is the standard difficulty in that literature. Rawls's natural duty of justice does not turn on strain either. Strain matters directly only to Singer's harm-prevention principle. So the claim that "all three depend" on strain is wrong for two of the three, and the convergence is produced by adding a condition that only one ground uses. The result is conditional with a trigger the essay says was not established. The conclusion ("those who draw on it have at most a light, defeasible reason") is then true but close to vacuous.
**Fix**: Say plainly that the three do not share a strain premise. Restate the shared element as benefit plus capacity to help at small cost, and treat strain as a separate amplifier that bears on beneficence and on the cost condition. State the conclusion as a conditional ("if benefit and capacity, then a light reason; strain would raise its weight"), and say what evidence would change the weight.

### W2: The best-evidenced costs are set aside while the argument rests on the least-evidenced link
**Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 3, core expertise but the point is a judgement about argument fit
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4 "so this cost lies beside the argument" and §5.2 "the argument of Section 3.3 does not make developers answerable for the cost"
**Problem**: The benefit that the argument relies on is training reliance, which Section 3.2 shows rests on a self-report and a 0.14% token share. The strain, in the evidence given, comes from other mechanisms: pageview loss attributed to search and answer products, scraper traffic ("at least 65% of the most resource-consuming traffic" from bots), and cleanup labour caused by tool users. These are cost-imposition, which a no-harm or fair-share-of-cost ground (not a benefit ground) can bear directly, but the essay twice sets them aside. The duty-bearer is also left unspecified: trainers, deployers of retrieval, scraper operators and tool users are different parties with different grounds, and "reusers" and "developers" are used interchangeably in Sections 3.3 and 4.
**Fix**: Add a short table or paragraph separating the parties and the mechanism that would bind each (training benefit; answer-product diversion; scraping cost; downstream tool misuse). Say which ground fits which. Either bring the cost-imposition ground into Section 3.3 as a fourth, better-evidenced route, or state why it is excluded.

### W3: The commons extension is conceded three times but never argued
**Severity**: Major (Minor if the target is a non-philosophy venue) | **Confidence**: 3
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "The step from these accounts to an epistemic commons is this essay's own and is open to the objections stated"
**Problem**: Frischmann's two goods and Hess & Ostrom give context, not an argument for why a practice (rather than a person or a just institution) can be the object of a pro tanto reason. Two points cut the other way. Ostrom's work explains commons survival through boundary, monitoring and sanction rules among appropriators, which outsiders do not hold, and the open licence removes those boundaries. And the essay's reading ("a reason not to leave its upkeep to others") is exactly the cooperative principle for beneficence in the fair-share literature (Liam Murphy, *Moral Demands in Nonideal Theory*, OUP 2000; Garrett Cullity, *The Moral Demands of Affluence*, OUP 2004; I can attest both exist, details from memory). That literature gives the extension a respectable home and the "light, proportioned, form left to the agent" structure the essay wants, without needing Hart or Rawls.
**Fix**: Add a paragraph on the cooperative/fair-share account of beneficence as the best fit for the premise, state what it needs (a collective effort, a fair share, a shortfall), and test whether Wikipedia meets those. Keep the flag that the extension is the essay's own, but give one argument for it.

### W4: The scheme's own terms are not used as evidence about fair terms (both as defeater and as support)
**Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 3
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "a gift to anyone under an irrevocable royalty-free licence creates no such claim for individuals", read with §3.1 "share-alike applies"
**Problem**: Fair play concerns the fair terms of a scheme, and the licence is the scheme's explicit statement of terms. It cuts both ways. As a defeater, a scheme that openly invites uncompensated use (every reader is a non-contributor) weakens a free-rider charge against any user. As support, contributors chose share-alike over CC0, which expresses a reciprocity norm, so the "gift to anyone" is conditional, not pure; this also bears on the Mauss point. The essay treats the licence as external to the normative analysis ("what the licence does not regulate").
**Fix**: Two or three sentences in 3.3 acknowledging both readings. Cite the copyleft rationale if a source can be read (for example Kelty's *Two Bits* or Coleman's *Coding Freedom*; search leads, details to be checked).

### W5: The fairness precondition is treated as a scale but is a threshold, and the essay's own evidence bears on it
**Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 4
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 opening "The changes below would therefore also strengthen such a claim, but they do not need it."
**Problem**: Rawls's condition is that the institution be "just (or fair)", which is a threshold. The essay treats it as a matter of degree ("strengthen") without saying so. And Section 2 and 5.3 report that newcomers are treated badly (Halfaker), challengers of the pillars are punished (Menking & Rosenberg), women's biographies are over-nominated (Tripodi) and oral knowers are excluded (Gallert & van der Velden). On the essay's own evidence the precondition is at least contested, which should discount the reason in Section 3.3; the text says only that it is "not assumed here".
**Fix**: State the discount explicitly (the reason is weaker to the degree the practice is unfair to those who join) and say that on the evidence presented the net effect is uncertain.

### W6: Residual gaps in the grounding of auditability
**Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 3
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "contestability is what makes the gaps visible and repairable", set against Tripodi's deletion-nomination finding in the same section
**Problem**: Two points. First, the claim that contestability makes gaps repairable sits beside evidence that the contest process (deletion nomination) is itself a route of bias; this should be answered. Second, Section 5.1 asks Wikipedia to carry the dispute record downstream so readers can follow it, but the essay gives no evidence that readers use such cues (it concedes Magnus's point that strategies are frustrated). Longino enters only through Menking & Rosenberg, and Fricker is "considered and set aside", so the framework that most naturally fits the gaps is deferred.
**Fix**: One paragraph on how contestability both finds and produces bias, and a sentence marking reader use of dispute cues as untested. Engage Longino directly (*Science as Social Knowledge*, Princeton 1990, which I can attest) or explain the omission.

### W7: Abstract and conclusion still assert what the body treats as contested
**Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 5, direct comparison of text
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "owe nothing under its licence by way of return"; "Language models rely on the practice"; §3.2 "Whether reuse strains the practice's conditions of upkeep is therefore not established"
**Problem**: Round 1 asked that "owe nothing" be aligned with "silent". The abstract's first sentence still says it. It is also a legal claim the body does not support: the attribution duty applies when material is shared, courts have not decided training, and two contract claims remain in *Doe v. GitHub*. "Language models rely on the practice" is stated flat in the abstract, the conclusion and Section 3, while Section 3.2 says the reliance premise rests on a self-report, a small token share and a contested literature. The conclusion's "modest and contested" also sits awkwardly with "not established". Separately, beneficence has no patient in the essay, since readers are conceded not to be harmed, and this should be said.
**Fix**: Replace "owe nothing under its licence" with "the licence is silent on"; qualify reliance ("are reported to rely on"); use one phrase for strain ("not established, with a plausible mechanism") throughout.

## Minor items

- **Secondary sourcing**: Klosko, the Arneson-style variant, Rawls on natural duty, Kant and Zwolinski are all through encyclopedia entries. The conclusion discloses this, which helps, but Arneson is attributed without an entry in the references. Read and cite Arneson, "The Principle of Fairness and Free-Rider Problems", *Ethics* 92 (1982), and Klosko, *Political Obligations* (OUP 2005); both exist, details to be confirmed. Nozick and Simmons have no page locators.
- **Hawley quotation**: I could not confirm the sentence "we may feel betrayed, but we have not been betrayed" in my search; check it against the Noûs text and give a page. The commitment account is also one of several; Baier's and Jones's goodwill accounts would count disappointed expectations of goodwill as betrayal. A sentence acknowledging the choice would suffice.
- **Frischmann paraphrase**: "non-rival until appreciable demand arises" inverts the usual statement (non-rival over some appreciable range of demand, rivalry arising at congestion). Check against the book.
- **Viljoen**: cited as a 2020 SSRN working paper; check for the published journal version (I believe Yale Law Journal, 2021) and cite that.
- **Cicero**: "the oldest statement of the sharing ideal" is an overclaim, since the lines are Ennius's, quoted by Cicero. Jefferson's 1813 letter to Isaac McPherson uses the same taper image for non-rival ideas and would make a natural link.
- **Teahouse versus Homepage**: Section 5.2 recommends "a named human response, as the Teahouse did", but the Newcomer Homepage included a mentor module and showed no retention effect. Describing it only as "tooling" hides that this is mixed evidence on the named-human remedy. This belongs mainly to the methodology and perspective seats.
- **Four requests in Section 4**: "acceptance of the terms the commons states for heavy use" needs saying who states them, given Section 5.4's criticism of unpublished terms.

## Questions for the authors

1. Which of the three grounds survives if no strain is shown, and does the essay then still claim any reason?
2. If the reason is not directed to editors, what remains of fair play, whose defining feature is that it is owed to fellow cooperators? Is the reason better described as a cooperative-beneficence reason?
3. Does the essay claim that scrapers, answer-product deployers and model trainers each bear the reason, and on which ground?
4. Does the essay hold that the practice currently meets the fairness precondition, fails it, or neither?

## Summary

The revision resolves or largely resolves the Cicero, trust, auditability, gift, literature and reform-section themes, and the C1 structural gap. It only partly resolves the conditional principle, the fair-play conditions, the commons extension and the duty-bearer. New or exposed problems are the constructed shared premise (W1), the mismatch between the benefit and cost mechanisms (W2), and the unargued commons extension (W3), with smaller items on scheme terms, the fairness threshold, abstract wording and secondary sourcing. A specialist would still demand a coherent account of what is left of fair play after disclaiming directedness and acceptance, and a better-fitting ground such as cooperative beneficence.
