## 2. Strongest Counter-Argument (about 270 words)

A scholar who rejects the essay would say the argument has been reduced to a statement nobody disputes and that the essay's own evidence defeats. The essay's core is that what deserves defence is an auditable community practice. Its evidence about that practice is mostly about failures of auditability: contestability is punished, newcomers are rejected by the machinery that gives reliability, deletion falls unevenly, oral knowledge is excluded, and the "community" that answers is anonymous and has no legal liability. So the object defended is an idealised practice that the essay's Section 2 shows only partly exists. Defending the ideal is circular. Auditability is defined by the essay, "not terms from the literature", then declared the thing worth defending, and Wikipedia is credited with it by definition.

The reuse argument is then too weak to bear weight. The reason to contribute holds only for those who "knowingly benefit", whose use "strains the conditions of its upkeep". The essay finds that strain "modest and contested". By its own account the antecedent is unsatisfied, so the reason may not exist. A "light, defeasible reason to contribute in some form" is satisfied by any donation, any link, any goodwill, and cannot be violated or tested. The reform section is generic transparency advice, much of it already in motion at the Foundation. Meanwhile the live rival views get a paragraph. Wikipedia is public infrastructure that should be funded or regulated, not asked for charity. Retrieval with citation can supply the audit link more cheaply. And free-labour critics hold that editors' unpaid labour is the wrong that matters. A thesis that survives every objection because it claims little is not an answer to "what is Wikipedia for".

## 4. Ignored alternatives and strongest rival views

1. **Infrastructure/public-good view.** Wikipedia, like other infrastructure, should be financed by states or levies on AI firms, not by moral reasons on reusers (Frischmann is cited, but only for the non-rival point). The "light reason" is a poor substitute for a funding mechanism, and the essay admits it.
2. **Substitution view.** Retrieval with citation plus automated citation checking may supply auditable provenance at lower cost, so the practice's special role could be a transitional phenomenon. The essay concedes provenance is no longer unique and rests on dispute record and answerability, which most readers do not use (its own Hardwig point).
3. **Free-labour / exploitation view.** Editors' unpaid work, licensed irrevocably and reused at scale, is the central wrong. The essay dismisses it by saying that judging exploitation needs "a theory of fair division that this essay does not supply", which is a refusal to engage, not a rebuttal.
4. **Deflationary view.** Wikipedia is mainly the most-linked tertiary source; the "auditable community practice" description is a philosopher's reconstruction of something most users treat as a convenience.
5. **Bias/capture view.** Evidence on ideological and structural bias in the practice (beyond the gender and geography studies cited, and beyond the one polarised-teams study) bears on whether the audit works. The essay cites only the favourable result.
6. **Collective bargaining/regulatory view** (data levies, mandatory attribution, opt-out registries). Acknowledged in one sentence in Section 6; not assessed.

## 5. Missing stakeholders

- AI developers: no account of their position or substitutes beyond "none of this shows they could not substitute".
- Cited-source producers (publishers, researchers, news). The essay says the dependence is "second-order", yet only the Wikipedia end of the chain is considered for reciprocity.
- Readers and editors in smaller editions (only Appendix A, from one edition).
- Biography subjects and volunteers whose data would travel in the dispute-record proposal.
- Affiliates and chapters, named but not weighed.

## 6. Unexamined premise

That the continuation of Wikipedia's current practice is a good the reader should help preserve, as against its being legitimately outcompeted, and that a diffuse set of reusers can be the addressee of a reason at all without a scheme that coordinates them (Singer's particularity point, which the essay raises and does not answer). If reasons to support a practice arise only where a coordination mechanism exists, then Section 4's "pooled arrangement" is a precondition of the reason and not an option for discharging it.

## 8. Observations (non-defects)

- Section 3.2 and the limits paragraph are now honest about the evidence; the remaining problem is that the abstract and conclusion do not reflect it.
- Splitting non-rival text from rival practice is the most durable idea in the paper and could carry more of the argument.
- The privacy cautions in 5.5 are a strength; they also show that the main proposal in 5.1 has a cost the essay has not weighed against its benefit.
- If the author wants a thesis that matters, one option is to make the claim about the dispute record: reuse severs text from contestability, and portable contest history is the one reform that responds to the language-model case specifically. At present that is one paragraph in 5.1.
