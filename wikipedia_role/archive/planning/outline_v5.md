# Outline v5: changing norms and impermanence, seen through Wikipedia

Status: working outline, 5 October 2026. Author's stated aim (5 October 2026): "social norms change; the impermanence of worldly affairs, seen from the fate of Wikipedia". Venue aim: a philosophy journal, so the paper must carry a real philosophical argument and not a policy brief.

## Working title

*Conditioned authority: impermanence and the fate of Wikipedia's norms in the era of language models*

## Thesis

1. **Wikipedia's authority is a conditioned complex of norms, not an essence.** It rests on credibility norms (verifiability, reliable sources), contribution norms (anonymous volunteer labour, open licence as gift) and reciprocity norms (readers visit, reusers credit, those who benefit do not hollow out the source). These norms held while particular conditions held: scarcity of trusted free reference, search referral that turned readers into editors, the cost of reuse, the comparison class against which Wikipedia was judged.
2. **The conditions have changed, and the norms are lapsing or being rewritten.** Its credibility status changed twice in twenty years (scorned, then relied on as a safeguard, then bypassed); the reciprocity norm that was tacit is now being written down (credit requests, paid access, preference signals); the community has rewritten its own contribution norms (the 2026 prohibition on LLM-generated article content).
3. **The reaction commonly called betrayal is the phenomenology of lapsing normative expectations.** It tracks what the norms had made reasonable to expect, and it does not by itself show that anyone broke a commitment.
4. **Three stances toward impermanence.** Eternalism treats the norms as timeless obligations (the moral-theory accounts of fair play, natural duty and beneficence read as attempts to find permanent ground; each fails at the premise that would bind reusers). Annihilationism treats them as mere conventions with no residue (the gift view: nothing is owed). The middle way holds that norms are conventionally real, arise in dependence on conditions, bind within those conditions, and can be deliberately re-conditioned. On that view what reusers owe is a conventional, defeasible reason that exists to the extent that the practice and its expectations exist, and the live question is norm design.
5. **Hold the function, release the form.** What matters is the function the norm-complex served: auditable testimony (provenance, contestability, answerability). Whether Wikipedia keeps its institutional centrality is secondary. The practical agenda is therefore function-preserving adaptation: make the record of dispute travel with reused claims, protect the way in for newcomers, widen what counts as knowledge by documented procedure, and design for succession (so that archives, licences and audit records outlive any one institution).

## Structure and budget (target about 6,500 words)

1. **Introduction (about 700).** The reversal (Middlebury 2007; the Foundation's 2025 statement); the claim that this is a lesson in norms and impermanence, not a story of decline; thesis; scope (English edition) and limits.
2. **Wikipedia as a conditioned norm-complex (about 1,300).** Credibility, contribution and reciprocity norms; norm theory (empirical and normative expectations; conventions); relational credibility (Fallis, Giles, Waters); auditability as the function (Hardwig, Menking and Rosenberg, Magnus, Tollefsen); internal norm change already visible (Halfaker on calcifying rules); the conditions of arising.
3. **The conditions change (about 1,200).** Licence silent; reuse at scale (Foundation-reported bots and scraping, with the interested-party caveat); readership and the contested evidence on contributions; Stack Overflow and Reddit as two other norm-complexes that changed in different ways; the community rewriting its own norms; the comparison class changing again.
4. **What lapsing norms feel like (about 900).** Reactive attitudes, resentment and betrayal (Holton, Hawley); Gouldner on reciprocity; what is known about editors' views (indirect); the gift view; tacit norms becoming explicit (credit requests, paid access, preference signals) as the mark of lapse.
5. **Three stances toward impermanence (about 1,300).** Eternalism: fair play, natural duty and beneficence as attempts at timeless ground, and why each fails here; annihilationism: the gift view; the middle way: conventional reasons within conditions, the pro tanto reason as conventional, norm design; the two truths as a way of holding conventional obligation without essence.
6. **Hold the function, release the form (about 1,000).** The function (Craig on the concept of knowledge as marking good informants; Hardwig); the portable dispute record; newcomers; widening by procedure; openness about agreements; designing for succession; what would count as success or failure.
7. **Objections and conclusion (about 600).** The lens is decoration; impermanence proves too much and gives no guidance; norm talk dissolves obligation; Wikipedia is not fading; a conventionalist account is too weak for a philosophy venue.

## Risks to address in drafting

- A lens used as decoration: each Buddhist concept must do analytic work (conditions, two truths, the two extremes) and be tied to a specific claim.
- Conventionalism too weak: state what a conventional reason can still demand, and why that is the right strength for this case.
- Overclaiming on norm change: the evidence of lapsing norms is mixed (the academic evidence on contributions) and partly from an interested party.
- Cultural framing: the term is Vietnamese-Buddhist in the author's use; keep the exposition anchored to anicca and dependent origination as they appear in scholarly sources.
